\section*{ABSTRACT}

Orbital optimization in valence bond self-consistent field (VBSCF) theory must accommodate nonorthogonal orbitals, prescribed atomic orbital (AO) supports, and relaxation of the structure coefficients. We formulate a matrix-free norm-extended optimization (NEO) treatment in support-preserving nonredundant coordinates, retaining orbital--structure coupling through independently expanded orbital and structure response spaces. The numerical study will compare NEO with conventional and block-preconditioned limited-memory Broyden--Fletcher--Goldfarb--Shanno (L-BFGS) optimization, with truncated Newton as an additional second-order comparison, reporting convergence, operator work, wall time, and peak memory under matched conditions. \section{THEORY}

Throughout this section, \(I,J\) label VB structures, \(\mu,\nu\) label AO basis functions, \(i,j\) label orbitals, \(n,m\) label electronic states, and \(k\) labels accepted optimization iterations. Boldface denotes matrices and vectors. Calligraphic \(\mathcal H\) denotes a matrix-free curvature or coupling operator, and square brackets denote its action on a direction. The superscripts \(o\) and \(s\) identify orbital and structure-coefficient coordinates: the first labels the output space and the second the input space.

\subsection{Wave Function and Energy}

The valence bond (VB) wave function for state \(n\) is written as
\begin{equation}
 |\Psi_n(\mathbf x)\rangle=\sum_I C_{In}|\Phi_I(\mathbf x)\rangle ,
 \label{eq:ansatz}
\end{equation}
where \(|\Phi_I\rangle\) is a spin-adapted VB structure and \(C_{In}\) is its expansion coefficient. The sum runs over the chosen structures, and \(\mathbf x\) collects the allowed atomic orbital (AO) coefficients. The Hamiltonian and overlap matrix elements are
\begin{equation}
 H_{IJ}(\mathbf x)=\langle\Phi_I(\mathbf x)|\hat H|\Phi_J(\mathbf x)\rangle,
 \qquad M_{IJ}(\mathbf x)=\langle\Phi_I(\mathbf x)|\Phi_J(\mathbf x)\rangle,
 \label{eq:matrixelements}
\end{equation}
where \(\hat H\) is the electronic Hamiltonian. The AO overlap matrix is denoted by \(\mathbf S\), with elements \(S_{\mu\nu}=\langle\chi_\mu|\chi_\nu\rangle\), where \(\chi_\mu\) is an AO basis function. Nuclear repulsion is constant at a fixed geometry and is omitted below.

For fixed orbitals, the structure coefficients are obtained from the generalized eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf H\mathbf c_n=E_n\mathbf M\mathbf c_n,
 \label{eq:eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
where \(E_n\) is the energy of state \(n\) and \(\mathbf c_n\) contains the coefficients \(C_{In}\).

In equal-weight state-averaged valence bond self-consistent field (VBSCF) calculations, the orbital objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E(\mathbf x)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{state}}}
 \sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{state}}}E_n(\mathbf x),
 \label{eq:stateaverage}
\end{equation}
where \(N_{\mathrm{state}}\) is the number of lowest-energy states included in the average. The choice \(N_{\mathrm{state}}=1\) recovers ground-state VBSCF. Orbital optimization minimizes this structure-relaxed energy with respect to \(\mathbf x\).

\subsection{Orbital Parameterization}

The AO expansion of orbital \(i\) is
\begin{equation}
 \phi_i=\sum_\mu b_{\mu i}\chi_\mu,\qquad \mathbf b_i=\mathbf R_i\mathbf x_i,
 \label{eq:orbitalexpansion}
\end{equation}
where \(b_{\mu i}\) is an AO coefficient, \(\mathbf b_i\) is the corresponding full coefficient vector, \(\mathbf x_i\) contains only its allowed entries, and \(\mathbf R_i\) embeds them into the full AO basis. A hybrid atomic orbital (HAO) has a prescribed localized subset of allowed AOs; an overlap-enhanced orbital (OEO) has full AO support.

Redundant orbital directions are infinitesimal invariances of the wave function that preserve the prescribed AO supports. These invariances include orbital scaling and admissible changes of inactive representatives. For a complete active-space expansion with full-AO orbitals, nonsingular changes of the active basis can also be compensated by changes of the structure coefficients. For a selected VB expansion, active--active mixing is retained unless it preserves the structure subspace.

The support condition must be imposed on the gauge generators themselves. If \(\mathbf G_i\) collects candidate gauge generators for orbital \(i\), and \(\mathbf F_i\) extracts its forbidden AO rows, admissible gauge combinations satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf F_i\mathbf G_i\mathbf t=\mathbf 0.
 \label{eq:supportgauge}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf t\) gives a linear combination of gauge generators that vanishes on the forbidden AOs. The complement of these admissible generators defines a local quotient basis. Local metric conditioning of the quotient basis produces a lift \(\mathbf U_k\) from reduced coordinates \(\mathbf p\) to packed raw coefficients.

The orbital update is written as
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf x(\mathbf p)=\mathbf x_k+\mathbf U_k\mathbf p,\qquad
 \boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf p)=\mathcal P(\mathbf x_k+\mathbf U_k\mathbf p),
 \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf x_k\) is the coefficient vector at accepted iteration \(k\), \(\mathbf p\) is the nonredundant orbital step, and \(\mathbf U_k\) maps that step to changes in the allowed AO coefficients. The preparation map \(\mathcal P\) includes orbital normalization and inactive-space projection used by the objective, together with admissible representative choices; \(\boldsymbol\theta\) denotes the resulting prepared orbital coefficients. The lift is held fixed within a local model and rebuilt after an accepted step.

Normalization of a raw orbital gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar{\mathbf b}&=\frac{\mathbf b}{\|\mathbf b\|_{\mathbf S}},\qquad
 \|\mathbf b\|_{\mathbf S}=(\mathbf b^T\mathbf S\mathbf b)^{1/2},\\
 D\bar{\mathbf b}[\delta\mathbf b]
 &=\frac{(\mathbf I-\bar{\mathbf b}\bar{\mathbf b}^T\mathbf S)\delta\mathbf b}
 {\|\mathbf b\|_{\mathbf S}},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:normalization}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf b\) is a raw orbital coefficient vector, \(\|\mathbf b\|_{\mathbf S}\) is its AO-metric norm, \(\bar{\mathbf b}\) is the normalized vector, \(\delta\mathbf b\) is a coefficient variation, \(D\) denotes a first differential, and \(\mathbf I\) is the identity matrix.

The trust-region norm measures the physical orbital displacement. Let \(\mathcal L_k\) map a packed coefficient tangent to AO-metric-weighted horizontal orbital features. For individually meaningful active orbitals, these features are the differentials of normalized, inactive-projected orbital rays, weighted by \(\mathbf S^{1/2}\). For inactive orbitals, and for the active orbitals of a complete full-AO active space, horizontal subspace tangents replace individual-ray tangents; inverse Gram factors remove dependence on the chosen basis within each subspace. Variations of the inactive projector are included in the active features.

On the nonredundant coordinates, the metric and its dual norm are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf G_o=\mathbf U_k^T\mathcal L_k^T\mathcal L_k\mathbf U_k,
 \qquad \|\mathbf p\|_{\mathbf G_o}^2=\mathbf p^T\mathbf G_o\mathbf p,
 \qquad \|\mathbf r_o\|_{\mathbf G_o^{-1}}^2
 =\mathbf r_o^T\mathbf G_o^{-1}\mathbf r_o,
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf G_o\) is the physical orbital metric and \(\mathbf r_o\) is an orbital gradient or stationarity residual. We assume \(\mathbf G_o\) is positive definite on the retained coordinates; its action is evaluated from the orbital features.

\subsection{Coupled Response}

The response derivation assumes that \(\mathbf M\) is positive definite on the working structure space and that the averaged states are separated from the remaining states by an energy gap. Degeneracies within the averaged manifold are allowed.

For an orbital direction, let a dot denote the derivative at the current accepted point. Stationarity with respect to the structure coefficients gives
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mathcal E}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{state}}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{state}}}
 \mathbf c_n^T(\dot{\mathbf H}-E_n\dot{\mathbf M})\mathbf c_n.
 \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
Here \(\dot{\mathbf H}\) and \(\dot{\mathbf M}\) are the directional derivatives of the structure Hamiltonian and overlap matrices, and \(\dot{\mathcal E}\) is the corresponding energy derivative. Structure response first enters the second derivative.

Derivatives in the reduced coordinates follow from the orbital map in eq~\eqref{eq:update}. For a scalar function \(F\) of prepared orbitals, the second derivative is
\begin{equation}
 D^2(F\circ\boldsymbol\theta)[\mathbf u,\mathbf v]
 =D^2F[D\boldsymbol\theta\mathbf u,D\boldsymbol\theta\mathbf v]
 +DF[D^2\boldsymbol\theta[\mathbf u,\mathbf v]],
 \label{eq:pullback}
\end{equation}
where \(D\) and \(D^2\) denote first and second differentials, \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\) are reduced orbital directions, and all differentials are evaluated at \(\mathbf p=0\). The preparation-map contribution is included in the orbital curvature action.

Collect the selected eigenvectors in \(\mathbf C=(\mathbf c_1,\ldots,\mathbf c_{N_{\mathrm{state}}})\). Differentiation of \(\mathbf C^T\mathbf M\mathbf C=\mathbf I\) fixes the symmetric selected-space component of their response. We choose the internal gauge
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mathbf C}_g=-\tfrac12\mathbf C
 (\mathbf C^T\dot{\mathbf M}\mathbf C),\qquad
 \dot{\mathbf c}_n=\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}+\frac{\mathbf Z\mathbf q_n}{d_n},
 \quad d_n=\sqrt{2/N_{\mathrm{state}}}.
 \label{eq:structurechart}
\end{equation}
Here \(\dot{\mathbf C}_g\) is the selected-space response required by normalization, \(\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}\) is its column for state \(n\), and \(\mathbf q_n\) contains the independent response coordinates for that state. The factor \(d_n\) accounts for the equal state weight. The matrix \(\mathbf Z\) spans the complement of the selected states and satisfies \(\mathbf C^T\mathbf M\mathbf Z=0\) and \(\mathbf Z^T\mathbf Z=\mathbf I\). Rotations within the selected equally weighted eigenspace leave the averaged energy unchanged and are excluded from the combined response vector \(\mathbf q=(\mathbf q_1^T,\ldots,\mathbf q_{N_{\mathrm{state}}}^T)^T\).

Writing \(\dot{\mathbf H}[\mathbf p]\) and \(\dot{\mathbf M}[\mathbf p]\) for orbital directional derivatives, differentiation of the generalized eigenproblem and projection with \(\mathbf Z^T\) give
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 (\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathbf q])_n&=\mathbf Z^T(\mathbf H-E_n\mathbf M)\mathbf Z\mathbf q_n,\\
 (\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p])_n&=d_n\mathbf Z^T\big[
 (\dot{\mathbf H}[\mathbf p]-E_n\dot{\mathbf M}[\mathbf p])\mathbf c_n
 +(\mathbf H-E_n\mathbf M)\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}[\mathbf p]\big],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal H^{ss}\) is the structure curvature operator and \(\mathcal H^{so}\) maps an orbital direction to a structure-response forcing. The subscript \(n\) extracts the response for state \(n\). The first action is evaluated through Hamiltonian and overlap actions on \(\mathbf Z\mathbf q_n\). The derivative of the eigenvalue drops out because \(\mathbf Z^T\mathbf M\mathbf c_n=0\). The factors \(d_n\) absorb the state weights and the factor of two in the real-wave-function coefficient Hessian. The gauge term in eq~\eqref{eq:coupling} is essential for a moving nonorthogonal structure metric.

Let \(\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf v]\) denote the orbital Hessian--vector product (HVP) after enforcing this selected-space normalization gauge and the orbital-map pullback, but before eliminating horizontal structure response. To define its bilinear form, introduce \(\mathbf K_n=\mathbf H-E_n\mathbf M\), \(\mathbf K_{n,u}=D\mathbf H[\mathbf u]-E_nD\mathbf M[\mathbf u]\), and \(\mathbf K_{n,uv}=D^2\mathbf H[\mathbf u,\mathbf v]-E_nD^2\mathbf M[\mathbf u,\mathbf v]\), with \(E_n\) held at its accepted-point value in these definitions. The second variation of the normalization-constrained energy gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf u^T\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf v]=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{state}}}\sum_n\big\{&
 \mathbf c_n^T\mathbf K_{n,uv}\mathbf c_n
 +2\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}[\mathbf u]^T\mathbf K_{n,v}\mathbf c_n\\
 &+2\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}[\mathbf v]^T\mathbf K_{n,u}\mathbf c_n
 +2\dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}[\mathbf u]^T\mathbf K_n
       \dot{\mathbf c}_{g,n}[\mathbf v]\big\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:orbitalblock}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf u^T\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf v]\) is the bilinear second variation in orbital directions \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\). The sum runs over the \(N_{\mathrm{state}}\) selected states. All matrix derivatives in this expression include the orbital preparation map. Second derivatives of the coefficient gauge cancel against the normalization multipliers at a stationary structure solution; the first derivatives of that gauge remain. The constrained energy expansion is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_k(\mathbf p,\mathbf q)=\mathcal E_k+\mathbf g_o^T\mathbf p
 +\tfrac12\mathbf p^T\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf p]
 +\mathbf q^T\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]+\tfrac12\mathbf q^T\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathbf q],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where \(Q_k\) is the local quadratic energy model, \(\mathcal E_k=\mathcal E(\mathbf x_k)\) is the accepted-point energy, and \(\mathbf g_o\) is its reduced orbital gradient, obtained from eq~\eqref{eq:gradient} in the coordinates of eq~\eqref{eq:update}. The reverse coupling action is denoted by \(\mathcal H^{os}\), with \(\mathbf p^T\mathcal H^{os}[\mathbf q]=\mathbf q^T\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]\).

For a separated lowest-state manifold, \(\mathcal H^{ss}\) is positive definite on the horizontal space. Define the response map \(\mathcal T[\mathbf f]\) as the solution of \(\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathcal T[\mathbf f]]=\mathbf f\). If this operator is singular, the minimum-norm solution is defined only for admissible forcing in its range. Eliminating structure response yields
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf q(\mathbf p)&=-\mathcal T[\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]],\\
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{rel}}[\mathbf p]
 &=\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf p]-\mathcal H^{os}[\mathcal T[\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]]],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:schur}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{rel}}[\mathbf p]\) is the relaxed orbital HVP, including structure-coefficient relaxation. The response map is evaluated by an iterative linear solve. Small structure gaps can make the relaxation contribution large even when the coupling action is small. A complete eigenvalue spectrum is not needed to evaluate eq~\eqref{eq:schur}; iterative actions on the admissible response space suffice.

\subsection{Norm-Extended Optimization}

The coupled quadratic model is minimized within an orbital trust region:
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\mathbf p,\mathbf q}Q_k(\mathbf p,\mathbf q)
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad \mathbf p^T\mathbf G_o\mathbf p\leq\Delta_k^2,
 \label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta_k>0\) is the trust-region radius at accepted iteration \(k\). The norm constraint acts on the orbital step. The stationary equations are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf p]+\mathcal H^{os}[\mathbf q]
       +\lambda\mathbf G_o\mathbf p&=-\mathbf g_o,\\
 \mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]+\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathbf q]&=\mathbf0,\\
 \lambda\geq0,\qquad \lambda(\mathbf p^T\mathbf G_o\mathbf p-\Delta_k^2)&=0,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:couplednewton}
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda\) is the nonnegative Lagrange multiplier for the orbital norm constraint. The last equality is the complementarity condition: \(\lambda\) can be positive only for a boundary step. Under the assumptions of eq~\eqref{eq:schur}, elimination gives
\(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{rel}}[\mathbf p]+\lambda\mathbf G_o\mathbf p=-\mathbf g_o\).
The exact minimum additionally requires positive semidefiniteness of the shifted relaxed Hessian. A small stationarity residual alone does not certify that curvature condition.

Norm-extended optimization (NEO) connects a restricted Newton step to a gradient-scaled augmented eigenproblem [1]. In the eliminated orbital space, this relation reads
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \alpha\mathbf g_o^T\mathbf y&=\mu\beta,\\
 \alpha\beta\mathbf g_o+\mathcal H_{\mathrm{rel}}[\mathbf y]
       &=\mu\mathbf G_o\mathbf y,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:neo}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha\) scales the gradient coupling, \(\mu\) is an augmented generalized eigenvalue, and \((\beta,\mathbf y^T)^T\) is its eigenvector, with scalar homogeneous component \(\beta\) and orbital component \(\mathbf y\). For \(\alpha>0\) and \(\beta\neq0\), the extracted step satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf p=\frac{\mathbf y}{\alpha\beta},\qquad
 \lambda=-\mu,\qquad
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{rel}}[\mathbf p]+\lambda\mathbf G_o\mathbf p=-\mathbf g_o,
 \qquad \alpha^2=\frac{\lambda}{-\mathbf g_o^T\mathbf p}.
 \label{eq:neoextract}
\end{equation}
Here the augmented eigenvalue determines the same orbital shift \(\lambda\) that appears in eq~\eqref{eq:couplednewton}. The unshifted interior Newton step is recovered as a limiting case; a zero homogeneous component or a hard case requires direct treatment of the restricted quadratic problem rather than division by \(\beta\). Our solver obtains the small projected restricted step spectrally and uses eqs~\eqref{eq:neo} and \eqref{eq:neoextract} to express its norm-extended interpretation.

\subsection{Iterative Solution}

Orbital and structure spaces are expanded independently. Let \(\mathbf V\) and \(\mathbf W\) contain the retained orbital and structure basis directions, respectively. We write the trial response as \(\mathbf p=\mathbf V\boldsymbol\xi\) and \(\mathbf q=\mathbf W\boldsymbol\zeta\), where \(\boldsymbol\xi\) and \(\boldsymbol\zeta\) are the corresponding expansion coefficients. The projected matrices are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf h^{oo}&=\mathbf V^T\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf V],&
 \mathbf h^{so}&=\mathbf W^T\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf V],\\
 \mathbf h^{ss}&=\mathbf W^T\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathbf W],&
 \mathbf G_V&=\mathbf V^T\mathbf G_o\mathbf V,\\
 \mathbf h_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\mathbf h^{oo}-\mathbf h^{os}(\mathbf h^{ss})^\dagger\mathbf h^{so}.&&
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf h^{oo}\), \(\mathbf h^{so}\), \(\mathbf h^{ss}\), and \(\mathbf G_V\) are small subspace matrices, \(\mathbf h^{os}=(\mathbf h^{so})^T\), and \(\mathbf h_{\mathrm{rel}}\) is the projected relaxed curvature matrix. An operator acting on a basis matrix applies independently to its columns. Curvature and coupling are evaluated by matrix-free actions, with only their small subspace projections stored as matrices. The dagger on the small matrix denotes its Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. New directions require new operator images; previously computed images are retained at the same accepted orbital point. Changing the trust radius does not invalidate these images. Full-space residuals determine whether the subspaces must be enlarged.

Certification uses residuals in the original retained coordinate spaces:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf r_o&=\mathbf g_o+\mathcal H^{oo}[\mathbf p]+\mathcal H^{os}[\mathbf q]
                  +\lambda\mathbf G_o\mathbf p,\\
 \mathbf r_s&=\mathcal H^{so}[\mathbf p]+\mathcal H^{ss}[\mathbf q],\\
 \big(\|\mathbf r_o\|_{\mathbf G_o^{-1}}^2+\|\mathbf r_s\|_2^2\big)^{1/2}
 &\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\eta_k\|\mathbf g_o\|_{\mathbf G_o^{-1}},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf r_o\) and \(\mathbf r_s\) are the orbital and structure stationarity residuals, respectively, \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}\geq0\) is an absolute residual tolerance, and \(\eta_k\geq0\) is the iteration-dependent relative forcing tolerance. The notation \(\|\cdot\|_2\) denotes the Euclidean norm; for the structure residual it refers specifically to the scaled coordinates in eq~\eqref{eq:structurechart}. Step feasibility and complementarity are checked separately. Curvature information from the projected space is not a general certificate of positive semidefiniteness outside that space.

A small structure residual can still give a significant orbital error when \(\mathcal H^{ss}\) is ill-conditioned. Where needed, we solve for a response correction and examine its orbital effect:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H^{ss}[\delta\mathbf q]=-\mathbf r_s,\qquad
 \mathbf r_o^{\mathrm{corr}}=\mathbf r_o+\mathcal H^{os}[\delta\mathbf q].
 \label{eq:responsecertificate}
\end{equation}
Here \(\delta\mathbf q\) is a correction to the candidate structure response and \(\mathbf r_o^{\mathrm{corr}}\) is the orbital residual after that correction. An unresolved correction supplies an additional response direction; a sufficiently accurate correction completes the candidate response. This targets the response needed by the current orbital candidate instead of solving every orbital basis direction to final response accuracy. Appending an orbital direction requires its orbital-to-structure image.

\subsection{Step Acceptance and Convergence}

After applying the orbital step in eq~\eqref{eq:update}, the structure coefficients are reoptimized at the trial orbitals. Acceptance compares the actual relaxed energy decrease with the coupled-model prediction,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_k=\frac{\mathcal E_k-\mathcal E(\mathbf x_k+\mathbf U_k\mathbf p)}
 {Q_k(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)-Q_k(\mathbf p,\mathbf q)},
 \label{eq:agreement}
\end{equation}
where \(\rho_k\) is the ratio of actual to predicted energy decrease. The numerator uses the reoptimized structure energy at the trial orbitals, whereas the denominator uses the local quadratic model. Accepted points receive a new true gradient, chart, and local model. Rejected trials retain the accepted-point operator data while the trust radius is adjusted. A previous orbital direction may provide a warm start; the curvature model is rebuilt at each accepted point.

The full coupled model must be distinguished from the production core-only approximation. The latter retains direct inactive/core response and the nonlinear pullback with fixed upstream adjoints, but omits both local active response and horizontal structure response. The current admission decision compares response-space dimension and estimated contraction work with orbital-space dimension and core work. This is a computational work heuristic, not an error bound on omitted curvature. In both branches, trial energies and accepted gradients are evaluated for the same relaxed VBSCF objective. Full and core-only calculations must be identified separately in the numerical results.

Near a smooth isolated minimum with a nonsingular quotient Hessian, the full model approaches an ordinary Newton step once the trust region is inactive. Superlinear local convergence requires the effective Newton residual to be \(o(\|\mathbf g_o\|)\); quadratic convergence requires an \(O(\|\mathbf g_o\|^2)\) residual, locally Lipschitz second derivatives, and correspondingly controlled structure-solver and derivative errors. A forcing sequence \(\eta_k=O(\|\mathbf g_o\|)\) supplies the relative part of this requirement, but a fixed nonzero \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}\) eventually limits the certified accuracy. These local conclusions do not automatically apply to the core-only model or establish rapid entry into the Newton neighborhood.

The iterative solver uses a one-electron block preconditioner. The comparison methods are block-preconditioned L-BFGS (block-LBFGS), which retains secant history with a block initial inverse, and truncated Newton with Hessian--vector products (TNHVP), which adds conditional HVP-based corrections to the block-LBFGS step.
