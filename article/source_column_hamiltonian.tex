\begin{abstract}
An arbitrary selected valence-bond (VB) structure space is generally not
invariant under orbital orthogonalization. This does not prevent exact
evaluation of its Hamiltonian and overlap actions through an orthogonal
configuration-interaction (CI) representation. The transformed wave function
must be retained, but its complete spin-string product array need not be
formed. We derive an exact source-factor representation, apply the
Slater--Condon one-body generators separately to the two spin factors, and
contract only the original products needed for the final structure-space
projection. The resulting equations preserve the selected variational space
without a closure assumption or intermediate amplitude truncation. Source
and channel tiling bound the numerical workspace. Optional spin-exchange
reduction and causal pruning of the exterior adjoint are derived separately.
The scope is the Hamiltonian/overlap action, its active-integral adjoint,
directional integral adjoints, oriented residual-generator adjoints, and
selected-structure coefficient-response contractions.
\end{abstract}

\section{The selected VB space}

Let $n$ be the number of active spatial orbitals, with $n_\alpha$ and
$n_\beta$ active electrons of each spin. The complete one-spin string
spaces have dimensions
\begin{equation}
 U_\alpha=\binom{n}{n_\alpha},\qquad
 U_\beta=\binom{n}{n_\beta}.
\end{equation}
An index $a$ identifies an $\alpha$ string and $b$ a $\beta$ string.
For a selected VB structure $I$, let $w_{ab,I}$ be its signed expansion
coefficient on the corresponding spin-string product, with all normalization
conventions included. For structure coefficients $\vv C$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 X=\Wa\vv C,\qquad X_{ab}=\sum_I w_{ab,I}C_I.
\end{equation}
Here $\Wa$ is a sparse linear map from structure coefficients to a matrix
of spin-string product coefficients. The matrix notation does not prescribe
a dense allocation: the map can be stored as sparse incidences between
structures and touched string products. It introduces no determinant-pair
traversal or separately optimized determinant coefficient vector.

The original support and its two marginal index sets are
\begin{align}
 \Omega&=\{(a,b):w_{ab,I}\ne0\text{ for at least one selected }I\},
 &t&=|\Omega|,\\
 A_\Omega&=\{a:\exists b,\ (a,b)\in\Omega\},&
 J&=\{b:\exists a,\ (a,b)\in\Omega\},\qquad r=|J|.
\end{align}
Support is determined by the selected structures, not by numerical
smallness or cancellations in a particular Davidson vector.
Using the Frobenius inner product, the adjoint structure map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wadj}
 (\Wa^*Z)_I=\sum_{(a,b)\in\Omega}w_{ab,I}Z_{ab}.
\end{equation}
Thus only entries in $\Omega$ are needed at the \emph{end} of a complete
action. Nothing in this statement permits dropping intermediate entries
outside $\Omega$.

\section{Orthogonalization without a closure assumption}

At a fixed orbital point, write the nonorthogonal active orbitals as
$\phi=\chi R$, where $\chi$ is an orthonormal basis of the same active
one-particle space. For example, if $O$ is their positive-definite overlap,
\begin{equation}
 O=R\Tr R,\qquad \chi=\phi R^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This is an evaluation-coordinate change, not a change to the physical
localization constraints or to the orbital optimization variables.
Multilinearity of antisymmetrized products gives the exterior maps
\begin{equation}
 T_\sigma=\wedge^{n_\sigma}R,\qquad
 (T_\sigma)_{a i}=\det R[a,i],\qquad \sigma\in\{\alpha,\beta\},
\end{equation}
where the row and column subsets contain $n_\sigma$ orbitals with a fixed
ordering convention. The orthogonal-basis coefficient matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 Y=\Ta X=T_\alpha X T_\beta\Tr,\qquad
 \Ta^* Z=T_\alpha\Tr Z T_\beta.
\end{equation}
The minor matrices $T_\sigma$ are mathematical definitions; the
implementation applies elementary exterior transforms without assembling
them densely.

Let $\Ha$ be the active Hamiltonian action in the orthogonal spin-string
product basis. The selected structure Hamiltonian and overlap are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restriction}
 \boxed{\quad H=\Wa^*\Ta^*\Ha\Ta\Wa,\qquad
 M=\Wa^*\Ta^*\Ta\Wa.\quad}
\end{equation}
An orbital-dependent scalar inactive-plus-nuclear energy $E_0$ contributes
$E_0M$ to the total structure Hamiltonian and is separate from the active
action $H$ in this note.
The variational energy is
\begin{equation}
 E=E_0+\frac{\vv C\Tr H\vv C}{\vv C\Tr M\vv C},
\end{equation}
with $H$ denoting the active contribution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:restriction}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact selected-space restriction]\label{prop:closure}
For any selected structure map $\Wa$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:restriction} gives
the exact selected-space Hamiltonian and overlap, whether or not
$\Ta\ran\Wa\subseteq\ran\Wa$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For arbitrary structure vectors $\vv u$ and $\vv v$, the two physical
states have orthogonal-basis amplitudes $\Ta\Wa\vv u$ and
$\Ta\Wa\vv v$. Their Hamiltonian matrix element is
\begin{equation}
 \ip{\Ta\Wa\vv u}{\Ha(\Ta\Wa\vv v)}
 =\vv u\Tr\bigl(\Wa^*\Ta^*\Ha\Ta\Wa\bigr)\vv v.
\end{equation}
Replacing $\Ha$ by the identity gives the overlap. No intermediate
restriction to $\ran\Wa$ was inserted.
\end{proof}

The nonclosure issue arises only if a different operation is used, for
example by zeroing transformed amplitudes outside the original selected
support before applying $\Ha$. That operation changes the wave function.
Equation~\eqref{eq:restriction} does not do so. Davidson vectors and Ritz
coefficients remain in the selected VB structure space; the orthogonal
representation is used internally to evaluate their images.
For linearly independent selected structures, their eigenproblem remains
\begin{equation}
 H\vv C=E_{\rm act}M\vv C.
\end{equation}
The adjoint $\Wa^*$ contracts against the selected test structures; it is
not an inverse coefficient transform. The nonidentity overlap $M$ is
retained, so this procedure does not replace the selected VB problem by
an unconstrained orthogonal-CI eigenproblem.

\subsection{A minimal nonclosed example}

Consider a two-by-two coefficient matrix with the single selected product
$X=e_2e_2\Tr$, and let
\begin{equation}
 T_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T_\beta=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
This is a linear-algebra example of the coefficient map, not an assertion
that a single spin product is a spin-adapted VB structure. Its transformed
amplitudes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:example}
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}ab&a\\b&1\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}a\\1\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}b\\1\end{pmatrix}\Tr.
\end{equation}
For nonzero $a$ and $b$, all four products are present, so the original
one-dimensional selected space is not closed. Nevertheless, two vectors
represent the complete $Y$ exactly. The overlap is
$(1+a^2)(1+b^2)$, not the value $1$ obtained by retaining only $Y_{22}$.
An exact Hamiltonian contraction must likewise use all transformed
amplitudes, explicitly or implicitly. The factorized route below retains
them implicitly.

\section{Exact source factors}

\subsection{Column decomposition}

Order the original touched $\beta$ columns as $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_r)$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 A_{:\ell}=X_{:j_\ell},\qquad B_{:\ell}=e_{j_\ell},\qquad
 X=AB\Tr.
\end{equation}
This is an identity for every input vector. It is not a truncated singular
value decomposition, and $r$ is not a fitted rank. Transforming only the
two factors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factors}
 L=T_\alpha A,\qquad F=T_\beta B,\qquad
 \Ta X=LF\Tr.
\end{equation}
The factors have dimensions $U_\alpha\times r$ and $U_\beta\times r$.
The complete transformed $U_\alpha\times U_\beta$ matrix need not be
formed, even if every one of its entries is nonzero.

The distinction between $r$ and the number of structures matters. A single
covalent structure with $m$ disjoint singlet pairs generally touches
$2^m$ original source columns. Several structures can share most of those
columns, and a broad selected space can touch all $U_\beta$ columns.
Thus $\rank X\le r$ is an upper bound, not an identification of $r$ with
one, with $\rank X$, or with the number of structures.

\subsection{A minimum mixed row/column cover}\label{sec:cover}

Grouping every source on the $\beta$ side is not always economical. View
the original input support as a bipartite graph: vertices are the touched
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ strings, and each supported product $(a,b)$ is one
edge. Choose a vertex cover $C_\alpha\cup C_\beta$, so that every edge
touches at least one chosen vertex. Assign an edge to its $\alpha$ endpoint
if that endpoint is in $C_\alpha$; otherwise assign it to its covered
$\beta$ endpoint. With this disjoint assignment,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover}
 X=\sum_{a\in C_\alpha}e_a u_a\Tr
   +\sum_{b\in C_\beta}v_b e_b\Tr=AB\Tr.
\end{equation}
Here $(u_a)_b=X_{ab}$ only for edges assigned to row $a$, and
$(v_b)_a=X_{ab}$ only for edges assigned to column $b$; the remaining
entries are zero. Thus no coefficient is counted twice, including edges
whose two endpoints are both covered. Each chosen vertex defines one
coordinate-anchored outer product.

Let $\nu(\Omega)$ be the maximum matching size, i.e., the largest number
of edges with no shared endpoints. A minimum cover has cardinality
\begin{equation}
 r_* = |C_\alpha|+|C_\beta|=\nu(\Omega)
 \le\min(|A_\Omega|,|J|).
\end{equation}
For completeness, the cover can be certified directly from a maximum
matching. Start at unmatched $\alpha$ vertices and follow alternating
unmatched/matched edges. If the reached vertex sets are $Z_\alpha$ and
$Z_\beta$, then
$(A_\Omega\setminus Z_\alpha)\cup Z_\beta$ covers every edge and
contains exactly one endpoint per matched edge. Any cover must contain
at least one endpoint of each disjoint matched edge, establishing
minimality. The implementation uses layered augmenting paths, with
$O(t\sqrt{U_\alpha+U_\beta})$ worst-case planning work and linear graph
storage. The plan is independent of the numerical Davidson vector.

This is a minimal factor count for \emph{support-only coordinate-anchored}
decompositions, not a numerical rank claim. In particular,
$\rank X\le\nu(\Omega)$; additional coefficient relations can lower the
actual rank further. A diagonal support needs one source per edge. In
contrast, the support
\begin{equation}
 \{(a_0,b_j):1\le j\le m\}
 \cup\{(a_i,b_0):1\le i\le m\}
\end{equation}
needs only two mixed sources, although either a pure row or pure column
decomposition needs $m+1$ sources. This reduces the factor dimension,
rather than enlarging a tile or imposing an amplitude threshold.

All subsequent Hamiltonian and sampling equations apply unchanged, with
$r=r_*$ and the factors of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cover}. When a common exchange
parity is used, construct the graph from the \emph{representative input}
support of Section~6.1; the final projection still uses the complete
original $\Omega$. If the original column cover is already minimal, its
ordering may be retained to avoid changing established summation order.

\section{Hamiltonian action on the factors}

\subsection{One-body channels and the complete active Hamiltonian}

Use real spatial orbitals and real, spin-independent molecular integrals.
Let $e_{pq}=\sum_\sigma a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a_{q\sigma}$, and use the
chemists' notation $(pq|rs)$ for two-electron integrals. The active
Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hsecond}
 \hat H=\sum_{pq}h_{pq}e_{pq}
 +\frac12\sum_{pqrs}(pq|rs)
       \bigl(e_{pq}e_{rs}-\delta_{qr}e_{ps}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $h$ includes the effective one-electron contribution of the inactive
orbitals. Define
\begin{equation}
 f_{ps}=h_{ps}-\frac12\sum_q(pq|qs),\qquad
 N=n_\alpha+n_\beta>0.
\end{equation}
Packed pair labels $P=(p,q)$ have $p\ge q$, so there are
$d=n(n+1)/2$ channels. Define the symmetric generators
\begin{equation}
 \hat E_{\sigma P}=\begin{cases}
 a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a_{p\sigma},&p=q,\\
 a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a_{q\sigma}
 +a^\dagger_{q\sigma}a_{p\sigma},&p>q,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \hat E_P=\hat E_{\alpha P}+\hat E_{\beta P}.
\end{equation}
Let $f_P=f_{pq}$, $V_{PQ}=(pq|rs)$ for $Q=(r,s)$, and $d_P=1$ for
diagonal pairs and zero otherwise. A symmetric kernel containing the
\emph{entire} active Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
 K_{PQ}=\frac12 V_{PQ}
       +\frac{f_Pd_Q+d_Pf_Q}{2N},\qquad
 \hat H=\sum_{PQ}K_{PQ}\hat E_P\hat E_Q.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\sum_Pd_P\hat E_P=\hat N=N\hat I$ on the fixed-electron
space. The second term in $K$ therefore gives
$\sum_P f_P\hat E_P$; the first term supplies the quadratic part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hsecond}. The usual integral permutation symmetries account
for both ordered off-diagonal orbital pairs through $\hat E_P$; no extra
off-diagonal multiplicity is needed in $K$.

Let $E_{\sigma P}$ be the matrix of $\hat E_{\sigma P}$ on its
one-spin string space. The Slater--Condon rules make this operation a
sparse signed occupation/replacement scatter, rather than a dense string
pair contraction. Its action on a product matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \Ea_P(Z)=E_{\alpha P}Z+Z E_{\beta P}\Tr,\qquad
 \Ha=\sum_{PQ}K_{PQ}\Ea_P\Ea_Q.
\end{equation}
These identities require a complete carrier for each fixed-spin sector,
or an exactly equivalent representation of all required intermediates.
Arbitrary \emph{selected VB structure spaces} need not be complete.
The two statements are different.

\subsection{Generated channel sweep and its adjoint}

Let $S$ contain the exact support of the transformed selected input and
$I=\bigcup_P\operatorname{supp}(\Ea_P S)$ its one-generator closure.
For a response $L$ and state $Y$, define the channel panels
\begin{equation}
 D^L_{zP}=(\Ea_P L)_z,\qquad D^Y_{zP}=(\Ea_P Y)_z,
 \qquad z\in I.
\end{equation}
The same generated paths supply the Hamiltonian image and its integral
cotangent:
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_L=\Ea^* (D^L K\Tr),\qquad
 G_K=(D^L)\Tr D^Y.
 \label{eq:tiled-channel-vjp}
\end{equation}
Here $\Ea^*$ is the signed occupation/replacement gather adjoint to the
channel scatter. The Hamiltonian image is not truncated back to $S$ before
any further consumer; its exterior-transform and structure adjoints perform
the exact final projection. The supplied support must contain both inputs.
For $L=Y$ the state scatter is the response scatter and is reused directly.
For distinct inputs, read the state into the consumed input halo and place
$D^Y$ in the slab that subsequently holds $D^L K\Tr$. After accumulating
$G_K$, that slab is reused for sigma. Thus a second input requires no extra
product array, channel slab, or worker-local tensor.

For two inputs with the same certified spin-exchange parity, the channel
sum may use one representative per exchange orbit. Its multiplicity is
two off the exchange diagonal and one on the diagonal; apply this weight
to only one Gram operand. This gives the full integral cotangent, while
the emitted sigma remains a projected-action representative until its
known parity projection is applied. Uncertified or unequal spin spaces
use the complete generated channel support, without an exchange quotient.

With $m=|I|$ generated channel targets and $b$ right-hand sides, the dense
channel-kernel and Gram contractions cost $O(mbd^2)$ each, in addition to
the sparse scatters, readers and final projections. The complete-carrier
order instead uses $m=U_\alpha U_\beta$. This optimization avoids absent
targets and repeated scatter; it does not lower the power of the orbital
count in the exact channel kernel. The returned integral cotangent needs
$O(d^2)$ storage, separately from the fixed shared numerical slab budget.
No higher-order density tensor is formed, and no closure of the selected
VB structure space is assumed.

\subsection{Joint orbital-direction action}
\label{sec:joint-direction}

At a fixed orthogonal evaluation basis the signed spin-string connections
do not depend on the integrals. Their orbital dependence has already been
transferred to $Y$ and the channel kernel $K$. For an orbital direction let
$Z=\delta Y$ and $\dot K=\delta K$, and define $A=\Ea Z$ and $B=\Ea Y$.
Differentiating the Hamiltonian image gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-direction}
 \delta(\Ha Y)=\Ha Z+\delta\Ha Y
 =\Ea^*\left(AK\Tr+B\dot K\Tr\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\dot K$ includes the transformed one-electron and two-electron
directions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:K}; it is not merely the two-electron direction.
The integral cotangent required by the orbital Hessian pullback is obtained
from the same two scatters:
\begin{equation}
 J_K(Z,Y)=A\Tr B.
 \label{eq:joint-direction-adjoint}
\end{equation}
State weights and the factor of two for real-state energy derivatives are
applied after this bilinear contraction. They must not weight $B$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint-direction} itself.

The equality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint-direction} eliminates the second
connection/halo traversal, the repeated scatter of $Y$, and a separate
physical image gather. It also replaces the two orbital-generator
cotangents of $\Ha Z$ and $\delta\Ha Y$ by the cotangent of their sum,
by linearity in the left argument. Both kernel multiplications remain
necessary; neither the $O(mbd^2)$ dense channel-kernel scaling nor the
selected variational space changes. In particular, this is not a
low-rank approximation or a relaxation of the Hessian accuracy.

Three shared channel slabs suffice: retain $A$ and $B$, form $A\Tr B$,
write $C=AK\Tr$ into the third slab, overwrite the now-consumed $A$ by
$B\dot K\Tr$, and add it to $C$. The same numerical budget is retained
by shortening the generated target batch when required. The extra slab
therefore does not imply an increased cache budget or an additional
spin-product array. Smaller batches can increase metadata overhead; a
single symbolic traversal does not guarantee a factor-of-two speedup.

For a certified spin-exchange parity, let $D$ be the diagonal matrix of
exchange-orbit multiplicities on generated target rows. The integral
cotangent is $A\Tr DB$. The multiplicity must be removed from the state
operand before forming $B\dot K\Tr$; the projected-image multiplicity is
then applied exactly once to the combined output. Unequal spin spaces
or uncertified parity use $D=I$.

The raw finite-orbital direction still contains the independent left
exterior-transform term:
\begin{equation}
 \delta(H\vv C)=\Wa^*\left[
       (\delta\Ta)^*\Ha Y+
       \Ta^*\{\Ha Z+\delta\Ha Y\}\right]
 \quad(\delta\vv C=0).
\end{equation}
This term is not eliminated by channel fusion. When accepted images are
not resident, a second right-hand side carries $Y$ while the state reader
has a structural zero in that slot. The joint outputs are then
$(\Ha Y,\Ha Z+\delta\Ha Y)$, supplying the left term without an
accepted-point replay outside the shared sweep. Optional diagnostics may
gather $\delta\Ha Y$ separately from the already-computed overwritten
slab; the production path needs only the combined image.

\subsection{Triangular directions preserve the generated support}

The support $S$ used above is the downward closure of the original touched
spin products under the upper-triangular exterior transform, not the selected
VB structure subspace itself. Write $P_S$ for its coordinate projector.
Every $e_{pq}$ with $p\le q$ either keeps an occupation unchanged or replaces
an occupied index by a lower index in one spin sector. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 P_S e_{pq}P_S=e_{pq}P_S\quad(p\le q).
 \label{eq:upper-support-invariance}
\end{equation}
For the Cholesky factor $R$, both $R$ and $\delta R$ are upper triangular,
as is $L=\delta R R^{-1}$. The coefficient direction is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \delta Y=\Ta\Wa\delta\vv C+e(L)Y,\qquad
 e(L)=\sum_{p\le q}L_{pq}e_{pq},\qquad P_S\delta Y=\delta Y.
 \label{eq:upper-support-tangent}
\end{equation}
Thus the same support $S$ contains accepted states, selected-coefficient
responses and their Cholesky representation directions. Their Hamiltonian
channel sweeps need its one-generator closure $I$, not a second closure
introduced merely by differentiating the upper-triangular transform.

Only the upper-triangular part of the residual-generator cotangent is
consumed by the Cholesky overlap pullback. For any residual $r$, including
one that does not vanish outside the selected space,
\begin{align}
 \Gamma_{pq}&=\langle r,e_{pq}Y\rangle
             =\langle P_Sr,e_{pq}Y\rangle,\qquad p\le q,\\
 \delta\Gamma_{pq}&=\langle P_S\delta r,e_{pq}Y\rangle
                  +\langle P_Sr,e_{pq}\delta Y\rangle,
                  \qquad p\le q.
 \label{eq:upper-residual-projection}
\end{align}
These identities permit a streamed physical overlap pullback to consume
residual panels restricted to $S$, without retaining a complete residual
carrier. They do not justify dropping the lower-triangular components from
an interface that promises the full oriented generator cotangent. In general
those components change under $P_S$ and can be nonzero.

This is an invariant coordinate \emph{support} for upper-triangular actions,
not closure of an arbitrarily selected VB structure space. Projection to
selected structure coefficients before a residual-generator contraction
remains invalid. The argument uses neither equal alpha/beta electron counts
nor a common exchange parity.

\subsection{Separation of the two spin factors}

Define the same-spin channel actions
\begin{equation}
 D_\sigma=\sum_{PQ}K_{PQ}E_{\sigma P}E_{\sigma Q}.
\end{equation}
They include the particular distribution of one-electron terms implied
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:K}; they are not independently assigned physical
$\alpha$- and $\beta$-spin Hamiltonians. Expanding the two generators gives
\begin{align}
 \Ea_P\Ea_Q(LF\Tr)={}&
 (E_{\alpha P}E_{\alpha Q}L)F\Tr
 +L(E_{\beta P}E_{\beta Q}F)\Tr\notag\\
 &+(E_{\alpha P}L)(E_{\beta Q}F)\Tr
 +(E_{\alpha Q}L)(E_{\beta P}F)\Tr.
\end{align}
The last two terms become identical after summation because $K_{PQ}=K_{QP}$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hfactor}
 \boxed{\ \Ha(LF\Tr)=(D_\alpha L)F\Tr+L(D_\beta F)\Tr
 +2\sum_{PQ}K_{PQ}(E_{\alpha P}L)(E_{\beta Q}F)\Tr.\ }
\end{equation}
The proof uses commutativity of different spin sectors, not commutativity
of generators within one spin sector. It applies to unequal
$U_\alpha,U_\beta$, unequal electron counts, ionic structures, and arbitrary
mixtures of selected structures.

\section{Direct sampling and structure projection}

Introduce the pulled-back factors
\begin{align}\label{eq:pullbacks}
 L_0&=T_\alpha\Tr L,&F_0&=T_\beta\Tr F,\\
 L_H&=T_\alpha\Tr D_\alpha L,&F_H&=T_\beta\Tr D_\beta F,\\
 L_P&=T_\alpha\Tr E_{\alpha P}L,&
 G_P&=T_\beta\Tr\sum_Q K_{PQ}E_{\beta Q}F.
\end{align}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:T} and \eqref{eq:Hfactor}, the needed original
product entries are
\begin{align}\label{eq:samples}
 m_{ab}&=\sum_{\ell=1}^r(L_0)_{a\ell}(F_0)_{b\ell},\\
 h_{ab}&=\sum_{\ell=1}^r\left[\label{eq:hsamples}
 (L_H)_{a\ell}(F_0)_{b\ell}+(L_0)_{a\ell}(F_H)_{b\ell}
 +2\sum_P(L_P)_{a\ell}(G_P)_{b\ell}\right],
 \qquad (a,b)\in\Omega.
\end{align}
For any Davidson input $\vv v$, build $X=\Wa\vv v$ and return
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final}
 (M\vv v)_I=\sum_{(a,b)\in\Omega}w_{ab,I}m_{ab},\qquad
 (H\vv v)_I=\sum_{(a,b)\in\Omega}w_{ab,I}h_{ab}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact factored selected-space action]
Equations~\eqref{eq:source}--\eqref{eq:final} evaluate
Eq.~\eqref{eq:restriction} exactly for arbitrary selected structures,
without constructing a complete transformed product array.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:source} is the exact decomposition into nonzero original
columns. Linearity gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:factors}, and expansion of the
Hamiltonian gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hfactor}. Applying $\Ta^*$ to each outer
product yields Eqs.~\eqref{eq:samples} and \eqref{eq:hsamples}.
All other final entries have zero
coefficient in $\Wa^*$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Wadj}. The final sampling therefore
omits no contribution to Eq.~\eqref{eq:restriction}.
\end{proof}

The contraction can be read as the following sequence:
\begin{equation}
 \vv v\ \xrightarrow{\Wa}\ X=AB\Tr
 \ \xrightarrow{T_\alpha,T_\beta}\ LF\Tr
 \ \xrightarrow{\text{spin generators}}\ \text{factor images}
 \ \xrightarrow{T_\alpha\Tr,T_\beta\Tr}\ (h,m)|_\Omega
 \ \xrightarrow{\Wa^*}\ (H\vv v,M\vv v).
\end{equation}
The middle objects are represented by factors and channel panels, not by
a selected-space approximation to the transformed wave function. This is
how Slater--Condon evaluation remains available without requiring selected
structures to form an invariant space.

\section{Two additional exact reductions}

\subsection{Common spin-exchange parity}

An optional reduction is available when the two one-spin bases and their
orbital maps coincide, and every selected structure has the same certified
transpose parity $\eta=\pm1$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \Pi X=X\Tr,\qquad P_\eta=\tfrac12(\Id+\eta\Pi),\qquad
 \Pi\Wa=\eta\Wa.
\end{equation}
Construct $X_{\rm rep}$ by keeping $a<b$ with coefficient $2X_{ab}$,
keeping diagonal entries with coefficient $X_{aa}$ for $\eta=+1$, and
setting the other entries to zero. Odd parity has zero diagonal. Then
\begin{equation}
 P_\eta X_{\rm rep}=X.
\end{equation}
Since $\Ta$ and a spin-independent $\Ha$ commute with exchange,
their original-basis composite $\mathcal A=\Ta^*\Ha\Ta$ also commutes.
Furthermore $\Wa^*P_\eta=\Wa^*$. It follows that
\begin{equation}
 \Wa^*\mathcal A X_{\rm rep}
 =\Wa^*P_\eta\mathcal A X_{\rm rep}
 =\Wa^*\mathcal A P_\eta X_{\rm rep}
 =\Wa^*\mathcal A X.
\end{equation}
The same proof applies to $\Ta^*\Ta$ for the overlap. Source factorization
may therefore use $X_{\rm rep}$, but the final output sampling still uses
\emph{both} halves of $\Omega$. The unprojected image of $X_{\rm rep}$
is not itself a parity eigenvector.

For a single structure with $m$ independent covalent pairs, this can reduce
the source count from $2^m$ to $2^{m-1}$. It does not imply that the touched
column count always halves for an arbitrary selected space. Equal spin
dimensions alone are insufficient; common parity must be checked from the
structure map. Parity is not a substitute for total-spin adaptation.
Without it, the unreduced factorization remains exact.

\subsection{Causal rows of the exterior adjoint}

Only $A_\Omega$ rows of the $\alpha$ pullbacks and $J$ rows of the
$\beta$ pullbacks enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:samples}. An elementary forward
exterior shear updates $z_j\leftarrow z_j+s\epsilon z_i$, with orbital
coefficient $s$ and fermionic sign $\epsilon=\pm1$. Its adjoint updates
$z_i\leftarrow z_i+s\epsilon z_j$. Starting from the requested final
rows, traverse the adjoint operations in reverse chronological order.
Retain an update exactly when its destination is needed, and then mark
its source as needed. Execute the retained updates in their original
adjoint order, including the diagonal exterior scaling.

For a row selector $Q_A$, this computes $Q_A T_\sigma\Tr Z$ exactly.
Every intermediate row that can influence the answer is retained. The
pruning concerns paths through a linear transformation, not closure of
the selected VB space and not the magnitude of an amplitude. A bitmap on
one-spin rows and retained shear-edge indices are sufficient; no product
array or dense compound matrix is required.

\section{Tiling, storage, and arithmetic}

\subsection{Streaming the exact equations}

Split the $r$ source columns into panels of width $s$, and the $d$ channels
into panels of width $p$. Different source columns are summed independently
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:samples}; the Hamiltonian action never couples source
column labels to one another. For each source panel:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
 \item Build its sparse $A,B$, and apply $T_\alpha,T_\beta$ to obtain $L,F$.
 \item For a channel panel $\mathcal P$, scatter $E_{\sigma P}$ on the
       corresponding factors.
 \item Stream channel panels $\mathcal Q$ to accumulate
       $V_{\sigma P}=\sum_QK_{PQ}E_{\sigma Q}F_\sigma$,
       where $F_\alpha=L$ and $F_\beta=F$. This is a channel GEMM on
       flattened spin-row/source-column panels.
 \item Gather $\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}E_{\sigma P}V_{\sigma P}$ into
       the same-spin images. Pull back $E_{\alpha P}L$ and $V_{\beta P}$
       and add the cross-spin dot products on $\Omega$.
 \item After all channel panels, pull back the base and same-spin factors,
       add their samples, and reuse the slabs for the next source panel.
\end{enumerate}
Finally apply $\Wa^*$ once to the accumulated support arrays. Source,
channel, and right-hand-side loops reuse shared backing allocations;
threads do not receive private copies of the carrier.

The explicit source/channel numerical slabs require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memory}
 O\bigl((U_\alpha+U_\beta)sp\bigr)
\end{equation}
storage, plus $O(t)$ support coefficients and images per processed vector.
Additional persistent storage includes the sparse structure map, one-spin
link/exterior metadata, and the $d\times d$ kernel $K$. The source slabs
do not include any $U_\alpha U_\beta$ coefficient array. This is a
workspace statement, not a bound on the total process memory.
For an overlap-only action no channel panels or same-spin Hamiltonian
images are required, and the factor workspace reduces to
$O((U_\alpha+U_\beta)s)$.

\subsection{Where a speed advantage is obtained}

Let $q_\sigma$ count elementary exterior shear edges. With a full channel
panel ($p=d$), a leading-work bound per input vector is
\begin{align}\label{eq:work}
 O\Bigl(&r(U_\alpha+U_\beta)d^2
       +rd(q_\alpha+q_\beta)+trd\notag\\
       &+r\sum_\sigma U_\sigma n_\sigma(n-n_\sigma+1)
       +\operatorname{nnz}(\Wa)\Bigr).
\end{align}
The terms describe channel mixing, exterior pullbacks, final sampling,
Slater--Condon spin links, and the initial/final structure contractions.
The displayed shear count is an upper bound; causal pruning reduces the
selected-row pullbacks. Forward base-factor transforms remain necessary.
For $p<d$, channel mixing keeps the same leading arithmetic order, while
spin scatters can repeat across channel panels by a factor up to
$\lceil d/p\rceil$. A smaller workspace does not change any equation or
variational space.

A generated-product contraction instead has channel-mixing work of order
$N_{\rm cl}d^2$, where $N_{\rm cl}$ counts the generated product
intermediates needed by that contraction order. When this support is broad
and $r\ll\min(U_\alpha,U_\beta)$, the factorized order replaces the
product dimension by a source-weighted sum of one-spin dimensions. For
$U_\alpha\simeq U_\beta=U$ and $N_{\rm cl}\simeq U^2$, the leading
mixing ratio is approximately $U/(2r)$, before other work is counted.

For broad source support, $r$ can approach $U$ and the factorized order
need not be faster. Both orders evaluate the same orthogonal-CI action.
An arithmetic planner may select between them without a memory-based
admission test for the physical selected space. Exact applicability to
arbitrary structures therefore does not mean that their costs are equal
or that selecting one structure universally yields rank one. There is no
claim of eliminating the exponential growth of the complete one-spin
spaces.

\section{Integral adjoints without a product carrier}\label{sec:factor-adjoint}

Let the bra and ket amplitudes have exact factorizations
\begin{equation}
 Y_\ell=L_\ell F_\ell\Tr,\qquad Y_r=L_r F_r\Tr,
 \qquad \mathscr E=\ip{Y_\ell}{\Ha Y_r}.
\end{equation}
Their source counts $r_\ell$ and $r_r$ need not be equal. At fixed factors,
the derivative with respect to the Hamiltonian channel kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-adjoint}
 \frac{\partial\mathscr E}{\partial K_{PQ}}=
 \ip{\Ea_PY_\ell}{\Ea_QY_r}=G_{PQ}.
\end{equation}
This uses the self-adjoint symmetric generators, but does not assume that
the bra and ket, their source counts, or their spin spaces coincide.

First contract the unacted ket factors into the bra amplitude:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-bra}
 W_\alpha=Y_\ell F_r=L_\ell(F_\ell\Tr F_r),\qquad
 W_\beta=Y_\ell\Tr L_r=F_\ell(L_\ell\Tr L_r).
\end{equation}
For each channel $P$, form the two channel covectors
\begin{align}\label{eq:channel-covectors}
 C_{\alpha P}&=E_{\alpha P}W_\alpha
            +L_\ell\bigl(F_\ell\Tr(E_{\beta P}F_r)\bigr),\\
 C_{\beta P}&=E_{\beta P}W_\beta
            +F_\ell\bigl(L_\ell\Tr(E_{\alpha P}L_r)\bigr).
\end{align}
Their dimensions are $U_\alpha\times r_r$ and $U_\beta\times r_r$.
Self-adjointness moves the crossed generator from the bra to the ket;
for example, $(E_{\beta P}F_\ell)\Tr F_r
=F_\ell\Tr(E_{\beta P}F_r)$. Thus no channel scatter on the bra
source family is required, even when it contains many panels.
The full kernel derivative is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-kernel-adjoint}
 \boxed{\quad G_{PQ}=
 \ip{C_{\alpha P}}{E_{\alpha Q}L_r}
 +\ip{C_{\beta P}}{E_{\beta Q}F_r}.\quad}
\end{equation}
To verify this identity, expand $\Ea_QY_r$ as
$(E_{\alpha Q}L_r)F_r\Tr+L_r(E_{\beta Q}F_r)\Tr$ and move each
unacted ket factor to the bra side of the Frobenius product. The resulting
bra contractions are precisely Eq.~\eqref{eq:channel-covectors}. This is
the reverse contraction of the same one-body channel graph used for sigma;
no product-space one-body response or higher-order density tensor is formed.

Only $\operatorname{sym}G=(G+G\Tr)/2$ contributes to symmetric integral
variations. Using the packed diagonal selector $d_P$ and the effective
one-electron matrix $f$ defined above, Eq.~\eqref{eq:K} gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta\mathscr E=
 \tfrac12\ip{\operatorname{sym}G}{\delta V}
 +\sum_P\frac{[(\operatorname{sym}G)d]_P}{N}\,\delta f_P,
 \qquad
 \delta f_{ps}=\delta h_{ps}-\tfrac12\sum_q\delta(pq|qs).
\end{equation}
Unpacking the symmetric pair entries with half weight on each off-diagonal
matrix element gives the full-matrix Frobenius gradients for $h$ and $V$.
The product and factor algorithms share this final kernel pullback in code;
they differ only in the contraction order that generates $G$.

\subsection{A bounded reverse sweep}

For each ket source panel, stream the bra factors to accumulate
$W_\alpha,W_\beta$ once. For each $P$-channel panel, scatter the ket
and these weighted factors, then stream the bra base factors to accumulate
the crossed contributions to $C_{\sigma P}$. Only after the complete bra has been
consumed, stream the ket $Q$ channels and add their Gram matrices to $G$.
Putting the $Q$ contraction inside the bra source loop would repeat the
leading channel GEMM unnecessarily; that order is not used.

Let $s_\ell,s_r$ be the bra and ket panel widths and $p$ the channel width.
The required numerical workspace, measured in doubles, is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 (U_\alpha+U_\beta)
 \bigl[s_\ell+2s_r+(2+\mathbf 1_{p<d})s_rp\bigr]
 +s_\ell s_rp+\max(\omega_\ell s_\ell,\omega_r s_r).
\end{equation}
It includes loaded bra/ket base factors, weighted bra factors, ket channel
panels, channel covectors, and one reusable source moment matrix.
When $p=d$, the ket $P$ panel also supplies all $Q$ channels; otherwise
a separate $Q$ panel is required. Here $\omega_\ell,\omega_r$ count temporary doubles per source
column used by the respective factor loaders, excluding their output views.
They vanish for the original exterior-factor provider; source generation is
sequential, so the larger loading scratch, not their sum, enters the bound.
The immutable metadata and the required returned $d\times d$ gradient are
separate. Both source
and channel widths can decrease to respect the budget, without changing
the represented amplitudes or a derivative tolerance.
Source and channel widths are chosen jointly by the repeated-work estimate
in Section~\ref{sec:factor-schedule}. A complete bra is retained when that
schedule makes it worthwhile; otherwise the same equations stream its panels.

With complete channel panels, the leading dense arithmetic is
\begin{equation}
 O\left((U_\alpha+U_\beta)
    [r_r d^2+r_\ell r_r d]\right),
\end{equation}
instead of product-carrier Gram work of order $U_\alpha U_\beta d^2$.
The source-pair term is essential and is not assumed linear in rank.
Self-adjointness permits exchanging the bra and ket before contraction,
placing the smaller source family on the ket side. One-spin link
traversals and panel regeneration also contribute; a smaller channel
panel can repeat these traversals. No universal speedup is inferred from
the leading dense terms alone.

For an exchange-parity reduction, a representative cannot replace both
amplitudes without projection. A physical bra with parity $\eta$ may,
however, contract an unprojected ket representative $Z$ when
$Y_r=P_\eta Z$ and the integral derivative commutes with exchange:
$\ip{Y_\ell}{\delta\Ha Z}=\ip{Y_\ell}{\delta\Ha Y_r}$.
The caller must establish this identity; the common factor kernel does
not infer a parity projection from equal spin dimensions.

\subsection{Directional factors and the integral part of an HVP}

Let $R$ be the nonsingular upper-triangular orbital map in the exterior
transforms and let $\delta R$ be its direction. Define the spatial generator
and its one-spin representation by
\begin{equation}
 \kappa=\delta R R^{-1},\qquad
 \mathcal G_\sigma(\kappa)=\sum_{pq}\kappa_{pq}
       a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a_{q\sigma}.
\end{equation}
The derivative of an exterior representation obeys
$\delta T_\sigma=\mathcal G_\sigma(\kappa)T_\sigma$.
This generator differentiates the CI representation; it does not replace
the physical nonorthogonal VB orbital coordinates or their finite update.
For $Y=LF\Tr$, with selected structure coefficients allowed to vary,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-tangent}
 \boxed{\quad\delta Y=
   (\mathcal G_\alpha(\kappa)L)F\Tr
   +L(\mathcal G_\beta(\kappa)F)\Tr
   +T_\alpha\,\delta X\,T_\beta\Tr,\qquad
   \delta X=\Wa\,\delta\vv C.\quad}
\end{equation}
The structure map $\Wa$ and its exact support plan are fixed. The last term
therefore uses the same support factorization with the structure-response
coefficients; it is not a projection of either orbital derivative onto the
original support. The two orbital terms generally leave that support and
retain every contribution through their transformed spin factors.

The response interface therefore carries $\delta\mathbf C$ in the selected
structure basis, not $\delta X$ as a full spin-product array. For state $s$
and an original touched product $e$, it loads only
\begin{equation}
 (\delta x_s)_e=\sum_I W_{eI}\,\delta C_{Is}.
\end{equation}
The incidences $W_{eI}$ and source cover are already retained at the accepted
point. This costs $O(\operatorname{nnz}W)$ additions and scalar products per
state, with $O(P)$ temporary coefficients for $P$ touched products, instead
of an $O(U_\alpha U_\beta)$ response array per state. The response columns
are borrowed from their owning structure-space calculation; they are not
copied into a second bundle. Both the integral-gradient derivative and
the oriented residual-generator derivative consume these same columns.
The optional Hamiltonian/overlap output projection is omitted when only
the response adjoint is requested. This changes neither the full orbital
derivative in Eq.~\eqref{eq:factor-tangent} nor the selected-space variational
problem. In particular, a zero-dimensional horizontal CI tangent does not
remove the normalization response or its gauge multiplier.

For each coordinate-anchored source $j$, write $Y_j=\ell_j f_j\Tr$.
Its selected-coefficient derivative changes only the unanchored raw factor.
For a $\beta$-anchored source $\delta f_j=0$; for an $\alpha$-anchored
source $\delta\ell_j=0$. The symbols $\delta\ell_j$ and $\delta f_j$ here
denote coefficient changes at fixed orbital map. Combining these changes
with the orbital derivative gives
\begin{align}
 a_j&=\mathcal G_\alpha(\kappa)\ell_j+\delta\ell_j,&
 b_j&=\mathcal G_\beta(\kappa)f_j+\delta f_j,\\
 \delta Y&=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(a_j f_j\Tr+\ell_j b_j\Tr\right).
 \label{eq:merged-factor-tangent}
\end{align}
Thus at most $2r$ factors are required, rather than the $3r$ produced by
concatenating two orbital families and a separate coefficient-response
family. No cross term $\delta\ell_j\delta f_j\Tr$ enters a first derivative.
This is an exact source identity, not a numerical rank truncation. For a
physical exchange-parity projection, apply the same linear projection to
both sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:merged-factor-tangent}, including exchanged
anchors and their signs. A coefficient-only tangent needs only $r$ factors.

A source panel loads its base factors and applies
the oriented Slater--Condon generator to the required spin factor only.
The same temporary is then overwritten by the coefficient derivative of
that factor, transformed and accumulated. No second factor panel is needed.
Unlike a Hamiltonian channel, $\kappa$ need not be symmetric: replacing it by
its symmetric part would give the wrong orbital derivative. The one-factor
temporary uses at most $\max(U_\alpha,U_\beta)s$ doubles for panel width $s$;
it is included in the loader workspace term of the preceding bound.

Write $\mathcal J(Y_\ell,Y_r)$ for the symmetric integral adjoint of
$\langle Y_\ell,\mathcal H Y_r\rangle_{\rm F}$, including the shared
$K$-kernel pullback. Since each physical integral variation is self-adjoint,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-adjoint-tangent}
 \delta\mathcal J(Y,Y)=2\mathcal J(\delta Y,Y).
\end{equation}
Thus the integral-gradient derivative needed in an orbital HVP can be
contracted from the directional factor source directly, without constructing
$\delta Y$ or any higher-order RDM for this contraction. If the accepted
and directional factor counts are $r$ and $r_\delta$, the leading dense work is
\begin{equation}
 O\left((U_\alpha+U_\beta)
  [\min(r,r_\delta)d^2+r\,r_\delta d]\right),
\end{equation}
plus the one-spin link traversals and source transforms. The count
$r_\delta$ includes both orbital terms and any structure response, including
the physical parity reconstruction when required. It must not be estimated
from the accepted source count alone.

There are two exact placements of a certified exchange-parity projector
$P_\eta$. If $Z$ is the accepted representative and $\delta Z$ its tangent,
self-adjointness and $[P_\eta,\delta\mathcal H]=0$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-parity-order}
 \ip{P_\eta\delta Z}{\delta\mathcal H Z}
 =\ip{P_\eta Z}{\delta\mathcal H\delta Z}.
\end{equation}
For an accepted representative with $r$ factors and a merged tangent with
$2r$ factors, the two source-count pairs are $(4r,r)$ and $(2r,2r)$.
Both have the same source-pair moment count. The first has less leading
channel-Gram arithmetic, but can repeatedly regenerate its larger directional
bra when it does not fit the common workspace. The second can keep the
accepted bra resident without enlarging that workspace. Compare the full
scalar-work estimate, including the actual planned provider loads, channel
traversals and Gram work, rather than selecting solely by the smaller ket.
The projector placement changes only contraction order, never the selected
VB space or an accuracy criterion. Equation~\eqref{eq:tangent-parity-order}
applies to the symmetric integral adjoint; it does not permit moving a
Hamiltonian through the oriented generator in a residual adjoint.

Equation~\eqref{eq:factor-adjoint-tangent} is the integral part of the HVP,
not its complete orbital pullback. The derivative of the exterior-map
adjoint, the full carrier residual, and the state-average gauge terms remain
required. The present implementation keeps those terms while migrating the
integral contraction to Eq.~\eqref{eq:factor-adjoint-tangent}.

\subsection{Oriented generator and residual contractions}\label{sec:factor-generator}

Define the orbital matrix $\mathcal D(X,Y)$ by
\begin{equation}
 [\mathcal D(X,Y)]_{pq}=
 \langle X,(a^\dagger_{p\alpha}a_{q\alpha}
                 +a^\dagger_{p\beta}a_{q\beta})Y\rangle_{\rm F}.
\end{equation}
It is the coefficient of $\kappa_{pq}$ in
$\langle X,\mathcal G(\kappa)Y\rangle_{\rm F}$ and need not be symmetric.
For $Y=L_rF_r\Tr$, form only the weighted single-spin panels
\begin{equation}
 W_\alpha=XF_r,\qquad W_\beta=X\Tr L_r.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator-factor}
 [\mathcal D(X,Y)]_{pq}=
 \sum_j\langle (W_\alpha)_j,
      a^\dagger_{p\alpha}a_{q\alpha}(L_r)_j\rangle
 +\sum_j\langle (W_\beta)_j,
      a^\dagger_{p\beta}a_{q\beta}(F_r)_j\rangle.
\end{equation}
If $X=L_\ell F_\ell\Tr$, each weighted panel is obtained from source-pair
moments, $XF_r=L_\ell(F_\ell\Tr F_r)$ and
$X\Tr L_r=F_\ell(L_\ell\Tr L_r)$. The final contractions traverse the
oriented single-spin occupation/replacement links. They are not symmetric
Hamiltonian-channel derivatives. For real coefficients,
$\mathcal D(X,Y)=\mathcal D(Y,X)\Tr$; this permits a transposed
contraction order but not an unqualified interchange of sources.

For the residual $Z=(\Ha-E)X$, the required panels can also be obtained
without $Z$ or $\Ha X$. Using the same-spin actions $D_\sigma$ and
the ket channel mixtures
\begin{equation}
 V_{\alpha P}=\sum_QK_{PQ}E_{\alpha Q}L_r,\qquad
 V_{\beta P}=\sum_QK_{PQ}E_{\beta Q}F_r,
\end{equation}
the exact residual-weighted panels are
\begin{align}\label{eq:residual-panels}
 Z_\alpha={}&D_\alpha W_\alpha+X(D_\beta F_r)
       +2\sum_P E_{\alpha P}(X V_{\beta P})-E W_\alpha,\\
 Z_\beta={}&D_\beta W_\beta+X\Tr(D_\alpha L_r)
       +2\sum_P E_{\beta P}(X\Tr V_{\alpha P})-E W_\beta.
\end{align}
Thus $Z_\alpha=ZF_r$ and $Z_\beta=Z\Tr L_r$. Replacing
$W_\alpha,W_\beta$ by $Z_\alpha,Z_\beta$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generator-factor} gives $\mathcal D((\Ha-E)X,Y)$.
The spectator actions have been moved to the ket using self-adjointness
of $D_\sigma$; the crossed term follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hfactor}.
Self-adjointness of $\Ha$ does \emph{not} justify exchanging the two
sources here, because $\Ha$ generally does not commute with an oriented
one-body generator.

The implementation reuses the factor/channel sweep twice: once for the
weighted panels to produce $D_\sigma W_\sigma$, and once for the ket
panels to produce the spectator and crossed terms. After same-spin gather,
the ket's raw channel buffers are reused for $X V_{\beta P}$ and
$X\Tr V_{\alpha P}$, then consumed by the crossed gather. No separate
channel scatter/mixing implementation or product-space residual is required.

With complete channel panels, the leading dense arithmetic is
$O((U_\alpha+U_\beta)[r_r d^2+r_\ell r_r d])$, plus the oriented
single-spin link contractions. Without $\Ha$, it reduces to
\begin{equation}
 O\left((U_\alpha+U_\beta)r_\ell r_r
  +r_r\sum_{\sigma}U_\sigma n_\sigma(n-n_\sigma+1)\right).
\end{equation}
Accepted-state transition densities use this estimate to choose between
factor moments and product-space links. Their planner does not reuse the
Hamiltonian-channel cost estimate.

A conservative numerical workspace bound for the residual contraction,
in doubles, is
\begin{equation}
 (U_\alpha+U_\beta)[s_\ell+(3p+9)s_r]
 +s_\ell s_r\max(1,p)
 +\max(\omega_\ell s_\ell,\omega_r s_r)+(t+1)n^2,
\end{equation}
where $t$ bounds the number of workers and the last term covers the small
orbital-matrix reduction and its temporary result. The required returned
$n\times n$ matrix and immutable metadata are separate. For a generator
without $\Ha$, the channel sweep and residual outputs are absent, giving
$(U_\alpha+U_\beta)(s_\ell+2s_r)+s_\ell s_r$ plus the same loading
and reduction terms. No per-worker spin/product array is allocated.

\subsection{Reuse of the normalization response}

Let $C$ contain the selected structure coefficients and $M$ be the
structure-overlap matrix. The solve-free normalization response has the
known internal state frame
\begin{equation}
 M_C=C\Tr MC,\qquad K_g=-\tfrac12 C\Tr\delta M C,\qquad
 A_g=M_C^{-1}K_g,\qquad Q_g=C A_g.
 \label{eq:gauge-frame}
\end{equation}
Retain $A_g$ from the small selected-frame solve rather than infer it from
the expanded numerical vectors $Q_g$. This identity requires no closure of
the selected VB space under an orbital transformation.

For a single selected root,
\begin{equation}
 a=-\frac12\frac{C\Tr\delta M C}{C\Tr MC},\qquad Q_g=aC.
\end{equation}
At fixed accepted active-space eigenvalue $\epsilon$, the integral adjoint and the
residual-generator adjoint are quadratic in $C$. Therefore their
coefficient-only directional derivatives are
\begin{align}
 \delta_C\mathcal J(Y,Y)&=2a\,\mathcal J(Y,Y),\\
 \delta_C\mathcal D((\mathcal H-\epsilon)Y,Y)
 &=2a\,\mathcal D((\mathcal H-\epsilon)Y,Y).
 \label{eq:gauge-adjoint-reuse}
\end{align}
These identities do not assume that the full-carrier Ritz residual vanishes.
Since the accepted orbital-map pullback is linear in these adjoints, their
coefficient response is also $2a$ times the already retained active-integral
adjoint. No new spin-source generation, Hamiltonian action or integral
contraction is required for this part of the response.
Given the already computed frame $a$, the reuse costs $O(d^2+n^2)$ scalar
work in the current active-integral representation, with no extra array
beyond the existing directional adjoint; $n$ is the active orbital count
and $d=n(n+1)/2$. This removes the carrier dimensions and source rank from
the coefficient-scaling update, not from the rest of the orbital HVP.

The selected-state multiplier contribution remains separate, as does the
directional derivative of the orbital-map pullback. Neither is removed by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge-adjoint-reuse}. The reused quantity is the accepted
\emph{active-integral} adjoint, not the total orbital gradient, which also
contains inactive-only contributions. For multiple states, retain the full
matrix $A_g$; the weighted diagonal adjoint totals alone do not contain
the required cross-state adjoints. The general coefficient-response
contraction remains necessary until those transition adjoints are available.

\subsection{Selected-structure response without a response carrier}\label{sec:factor-response}

At a fixed orbital point let $Y_i=\Ta\Wa\vv C_i$ and
$Q_i=\Ta\Wa\vv q_i$, where $\vv q_i$ is a coefficient response in
the original selected structure space. Write
$\mathcal J_i=\mathcal J(Y_i,Y_i)$ and
$\Gamma_i=2\mathcal D((\Ha-E_i)Y_i,Y_i)$ for its unweighted accepted
integral and exterior-generator adjoints. With $E_i$ fixed, their
coefficient-response contributions are exactly
\begin{align}\label{eq:factor-structure-response}
 \delta\mathcal J_i&=2\mathcal J(Q_i,Y_i),\\
 \delta\Gamma_i&=2\mathcal D((\Ha-E_i)Q_i,Y_i)
                    +2\mathcal D((\Ha-E_i)Y_i,Q_i).
\end{align}
The first residual is the response of the wave function; the second is the
response of the generator's right argument. Neither residual may be
projected into the selected structure space before its generator contraction.
Both are evaluated by the residual panels of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual-panels}. Thus no $Q_i$, $\Ha Q_i$, or response
residual product array is required. Each raw response is generated once
on $\Omega$ and reused by the spin-factor adjoints and the tiled
$\Wa^*\Ta^*\Ha Q_i$, $\Wa^*\Ta^*Q_i$ projections.

\subsection{Product-tile response with resident accepted data}

For a broad source graph the generated product order can be cheaper than
factor-pair moments. Let $R_i=(\Ha-E_i)Y_i$ be the retained accepted residual.
The transpose identity for real one-body generators is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D(L,R)=\mathcal D(R,L)\Tr,
 \qquad (e_{pq})\Tr=e_{qp}.
\end{equation}
It rewrites the coefficient-response generator cotangent as
\begin{equation}
 \delta\Gamma_i=2\bigl[
   \mathcal D(\Ha Q_i,Y_i)-E_i\mathcal D(Q_i,Y_i)
   +\mathcal D(Q_i,R_i)\Tr\bigr].
 \label{eq:product-response-generator}
\end{equation}
Every contraction now has a generated response panel as its left argument
and a resident accepted array as its right argument. There is no need to
build a complete $Q_i$, $\Ha Q_i$, or response residual array, nor to
recompute $\Ha Y_i$. For disjoint beta-column projectors $P_t$, linearity
gives $\mathcal D(L,R)=\sum_t\mathcal D(P_tL,R)$. The two input-panel
terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:product-response-generator} share their signed-link
traversal; their second term is transposed only \emph{after} its oriented
contraction. Truncating its lower triangle before transposition would lose
required upper-triangular information.

The generated Hamiltonian sweep also forms the channel Gram for
$\mathcal J(Q_i,Y_i)$. Each emitted Hamiltonian panel is consumed both by
the first term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:product-response-generator} and by the
exterior/structure adjoint, before its storage is reused. Cache eviction
may emit partial Hamiltonian panels: linearity of both consumers makes
this exact without waiting for a complete sigma column. The physical
Cholesky pullback needs only the upper triangle. By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:upper-residual-projection}, the first term may therefore
consume the Hamiltonian image restricted to the generated support $S$.
This restriction does not assert closure in the selected structure basis.
For a common exchange parity, the same summed-spin generators commute with
the exchange projector, so the projected-action representative is sufficient
for this physical cotangent as well as for the structure image.

For a left panel whose beta columns belong to $B_t$, the two spin contributions
to an oriented generator cotangent can be contracted as
\begin{align}
 \mathcal D^\alpha_{pq}(L,R)&=
  \sum_{aa'}(e^\alpha_{pq})_{aa'}
        \sum_{b\in B_t}L_{ab}R_{a'b},\\
 \mathcal D^\beta_{pq}(L,R)&=
  \sum_{b\in B_t,b'}(e^\beta_{pq})_{bb'}
        \sum_aL_{ab}R_{ab'}.
 \label{eq:spectator-first-generator}
\end{align}
Each signed link is visited outside the spectator dot product.
Let $\ell_\sigma$ count diagonal and single replacements per spin string.
The metadata work per right-hand side changes from
\[
 O\bigl(U_\alpha |B_t|(\ell_\alpha+\ell_\beta)\bigr)
 \quad\text{to}\quad
 O\bigl(U_\alpha\ell_\alpha+|B_t|\ell_\beta\bigr).
\]
The numerical dot-product work retains the former order; this is a reduction
of repeated indexing and branching, not a lower orbital power for the entire
contraction. Beta contributions use contiguous column dots and alpha
contributions borrow strided row views, with no transposed carrier copy.
The implementation partitions alpha rows and beta link events separately,
so even one beta column can share its long spectator contractions across
the existing OpenMP team. Only orbital-matrix reductions are partition-local.
Upper-triangle selection is performed after the requested transpose and
outside the spectator dot. Exact-zero stripes may be skipped, but no
amplitude threshold or norm-based small-vector rejection is used.

\paragraph{Compact symbolic panels.}
For a beta panel $B_t$, let
$S_t=\{(a,b)\in S:b\in B_t\}$.
The retained input and partial-image panels store only $|S_t|b$
floating-point values for $b$ simultaneous right-hand sides, together with
one shared row-index pattern of size $O(|S_t|+|B_t|)$. All entries in this
pattern are retained, including exact zeros and arbitrarily small values.
This is symbolic support compression, not numerical sparsification.
The Slater--Condon gather adds directly into these indexed rows. An ordered
exterior adjoint temporarily unpacks one bounded panel; it does not unpack
the complete carrier. Full symbolic support uses implicit row indices.
Both caches retain their byte limits, and partial-image eviction remains
linear as above. Shared patterns are held only by resident panels, rather
than by an additional unbounded support table.

For a complete covalent family with $n=2m$ orbitals and $m$ electrons per
spin, the generated support obeys the prefix condition
$N_\alpha(k)+N_\beta(k)\geq k$ for every $k$.
Its cardinality is
\begin{equation}
 |S_{\mathrm{cov}}|=
 \binom{2m}{m}^{\!2}-\binom{2m}{m+1}^{\!2}.
 \label{eq:covalent-panel-count}
\end{equation}
To count the excluded pairs, reflect both occupation bits,
$(a_j,b_j)\mapsto(1-b_j,1-a_j)$, up to the first prefix with
$N_\alpha(k)+N_\beta(k)=k-1$. This maps an excluded pair bijectively to
two strings with $m+1$ electrons; the inverse reflects up to the first
prefix excess of one. Any selected covalent subset has support contained
in this set. Thus the complete-covalent upper bounds for 12 and 14 orbitals
are 226512 and 2760615 stored coordinates, respectively, rather than
853776 and 11778624 complete-product coordinates. This bound does not
apply to arbitrary ionic selections: their own generated support determines
the retained count, and the same bounded-cache algorithm still applies.

Cache misses may occur inside the persistent sigma parallel region.
The ordered exterior kernels therefore share its existing workers through
disjoint-column or disjoint-row tasks, instead of opening a nested parallel
region that executes serially. Each task borrows the same panel; there is
no per-worker floating-point carrier. This changes scheduling, not the
order of elementary shears acting on any coefficient.

Accepted arrays $Y_i$ and $R_i$ are borrowed, not copied. Removing their
storage is a separate migration of accepted and orbital-direction consumers;
the present identities remove the additional \emph{response} carriers.
Original-support incidence/value buffers, bounded column caches, small
orbital reductions and the active-integral cotangent have separate storage
costs. In particular, a touched-support buffer is not a guarantee of
subquadratic memory if the original support itself fills the product grid.

For positive state weights $w_i$ the contributions above are summed with $w_i$.
The separate overlap-multiplier term is
\begin{equation}
 2\sum_{ij} w_i\mu_{ji}\mathcal D(Y_j,Y_i),\qquad
 \mu_{ji}=\frac12\left[\lambda_{ji}+\frac{w_j}{w_i}\lambda_{ij}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{ij}$ are the directional normalization/orthogonality multipliers
in the structure-response equations. This weight-symmetrized gauge
contribution is not part of either residual panel.
An exactly zero $\vv q_i$ contributes no images or derivative tensors;
this follows from linearity, not an amplitude threshold.

For source counts $r_\ell,r_r$, the leading dense work of the integral
adjoint and both residual-generator contractions is
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl((U_\alpha+U_\beta)
        [r_r d^2+r_\ell r_r d]\bigr).
\end{equation}
The two Hamiltonian images also supply their generator adjoints by the
shared image consumer in Section~\ref{sec:source-state}. Only $\Ha Q_i$
is projected to structure H/M images; $\Ha Y_i$ is consumed by its adjoint
without an unused structure projection. With equal
representative source counts $r_r=r$ and physical bra count $r_\ell=l$,
the leading multiply--add count used for this consumer is
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm source}=6(U_\alpha+U_\beta)rd^2
            +(U_\alpha+U_\beta)lr(12d+20).
\end{equation}
The coefficients count one integral adjoint and two shared H-image/generator
adjoint sweeps. The energy shifts use the transpose identity for the ordinary
generator adjoint. The fused product sigma/Gram has leading
work $4N_{\rm product}d^2$, with $N_{\rm product}$ reduced only by a
certified exchange parity. Comparing these leading operation counts
does not fit timings or change numerical thresholds. Link work,
source transforms, sampling and finite tile effects still affect wall time.
A product order
can still be cheaper for broad support; this is an exact arithmetic-order
choice, not a change of variational space or a memory admission criterion.

\subsection{Accepted states and orbital directions without product arrays}\label{sec:source-state}

Let $F=\Ta\Wa$, $Y=F\vv C$ and
$\kappa=\delta R R^{-1}$. Holding the structure coefficients fixed,
\begin{align}\label{eq:source-direction-images}
 \delta(H\vv C)&=F^*\bigl[\delta\Ha Y+
               \Ha\mathcal G(\kappa)Y+
               \mathcal G(\kappa)^*\Ha Y\bigr],\\
 \delta(M\vv C)&=F^*\bigl[\mathcal G(\kappa)+
                               \mathcal G(\kappa)^*\bigr]Y.
\end{align}
The left-generator term differentiates the projection, not the physical
selected structure map. It may not be omitted even if the selected
coefficients solve their generalized eigenproblem.

Each Hamiltonian image is a sum of outer products from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hfactor}.
For any such term $AB\Tr$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal G(\kappa)^*(AB\Tr)=
   [\mathcal G_\alpha(\kappa\Tr)A]B\Tr
   +A[\mathcal G_\beta(\kappa\Tr)B]\Tr.
\end{equation}
Apply the oriented spin generators to the factor panels, perform the same
causal exterior adjoints, and sample only $\Omega$. Two additional shared
spin-factor channel slabs suffice, including both base and same-spin
images. They are reserved inside the original numerical budget, never
allocated independently per worker.

For a general orbital/structure tangent $\delta Y$, the accepted generator
adjoint $\Gamma=2\mathcal D((\Ha-E)Y,Y)$ has direction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-generator-direction}
 \delta\Gamma=2\mathcal D((\Ha-E)\delta Y,Y)
             +2\mathcal D((\Ha-E)Y,\delta Y)
             +2\mathcal D(\delta\Ha Y,Y),
\end{equation}
before adding the separate normalization/orthogonality multipliers.
Here $E$ is held fixed; its required directional multiplier terms follow
the same state-average gauge convention as Section~\ref{sec:factor-response}.
The third term uses the residual-panel kernel with Hamiltonian $\delta\Ha$
and shift zero. The integral direction remains $2\mathcal J(\delta Y,Y)$.
Consequently the accepted adjoints and their complete directions require
only structure coefficients, source providers, small orbital tensors and
bounded numerical panels, not accepted amplitudes or residual product arrays.
The differentiated Cholesky/integral pullback is still applied afterwards;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:source-generator-direction} does not replace that chain rule.

Computing Eq.~\eqref{eq:source-generator-direction} after releasing the
directional H/M images would repeat their Hamiltonian work. Instead consume
each outer-product image $X=\sum_j A_jB_j\Tr$ before its exterior projection.
For right factors $Y_r=L_rF_r\Tr$, accumulate
\begin{equation}
 Z_\alpha\mathrel{+}=A(B\Tr F_r),\qquad
 Z_\beta\mathrel{+}=B(A\Tr L_r).
\end{equation}
After all source/channel tiles, these are $XF_r$ and $X\Tr L_r$;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generator-factor} then returns $\mathcal D(X,Y_r)$.
For a certified exchange representative, accumulate its physical image
$P_\eta X=\tfrac12(X+\eta X\Tr)$ by adding the exchanged factors with
weight $\eta/2$. No product-space symmetrization is formed.

Let $D=\mathcal D(Y,\delta Y)$. Since
$\mathcal D(\delta Y,Y)=D\Tr$, the orbital-direction cache is only
\begin{equation}
 \delta\Gamma=2\bigl[
     \mathcal D(\Ha\delta Y,Y)+\mathcal D(\delta\Ha Y,Y)
    +\mathcal D(\Ha Y,\delta Y)-E(D+D\Tr)\bigr].
\end{equation}
The three Hamiltonian images are already generated for
Eq.~\eqref{eq:source-direction-images}. Their generator adjoints are
collected in that sweep; the subsequent HVP pullback reuses this small
$n\times n$ covector, not a sigma or residual product array. With state
averaging, the cache is the weighted sum and coefficient-response/gauge
terms are added separately. The integral-adjoint direction remains required.

One numerical budget is split between the H-image workspace and the right
factor/moment workspace. Right factors are tiled if necessary; the H source
can then replay, but its structure projection occurs only on the first pass.
When all right factors fit, the three images visit $4r$ representative source
columns: $2r$ for $\Ha\delta Y$, $r$ for $\delta\Ha Y$, and $r$ for
$\Ha Y$. The unfused residual-panel route plus the images visits $12r$.
This removes repeated channel mixing, not the source-pair moment arithmetic
or the outer optimizer's HVP calls.

\subsection{Joint source/channel scheduling}\label{sec:factor-schedule}

For source widths $s_\ell,s_r$ and channel width $p$, define
\begin{equation}
 N_\ell=\left\lceil r_\ell/s_\ell\right\rceil,\qquad
 N_r=\left\lceil r_r/s_r\right\rceil,\qquad
 N_P=\left\lceil d/p\right\rceil,\qquad U=U_\alpha+U_\beta.
\end{equation}
The leading dense channel and moment arithmetic does not depend on these
widths, but source regeneration, link traversal and output traffic do.
In particular, the number of loaded bra source columns is
\begin{equation}
 C_\ell=\begin{cases}
 r_\ell,&s_\ell=r_\ell,\\
 N_r(N_P+b)r_\ell,&s_\ell<r_\ell,
 \end{cases}\qquad
 b=\begin{cases}1,&\text{integral adjoint},\\2,&\text{residual generator}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here $b$ counts the channel-independent moment sweeps. If generating a
source column costs $\ell_\ell$ nominal scalar operations and element
touches, source work is $C_\ell\ell_\ell$. Each provider declares this work
from its sparse writes, diagonal scales and exterior shear multiply--adds;
directional providers add the oriented generator work. This metadata affects
only the evaluation schedule, never the amplitudes, ranks or tolerances.

The single-replacement links are generated in ascending packed-channel
order. For each larger orbital index, ascending lower indices with opposite
occupation generate every link once. A channel tile then uses binary ranges
in the existing link and occupation arrays. It does not scan all links and
reject those outside the tile, nor allocate a string-by-channel offset table.
Let $J=\sum_\sigma U_\sigma n_\sigma(n-n_\sigma+1)$ count the single-spin
occupation/replacement links. Across all source columns, matching-link visits
scale as $r_rv(N_P)J$, where
\begin{equation}
 v_I(N_P)=N_P+1,\qquad v_R(N_P)=2N_P+3.
\end{equation}
Without channel ranges these counts were respectively $N_P^2+N_P$ and
$2N_P^2+3N_P$. Binary lookup comparisons still repeat with those latter
counts and are included through an upper comparison estimate $B$ per
complete single-spin row family. The complete-channel case needs no binary
lookup penalty.

The schedule also counts repeated output reads and writes in the moment
GEMMs, and repeated channel zero fills:
\begin{align}
 W_{\rm out}&=2U r_r(d+b)N_\ell,\\
 W_{0,I}&=U r_r d(N_P+1),&
 W_{0,R}&=2U r_r dN_P.
\end{align}
Combining these terms gives the nominal variable-work estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat W=C_\ell\ell_\ell+
   r_r\bigl[3v(N_P)J+s(N_P)B\bigr]+W_0+W_{\rm out},
\end{equation}
where $s_I=N_P^2+N_P$, $s_R=2N_P^2+3N_P$, and the factor three counts
one matching-link visit plus its multiply and add. Terms invariant across
the candidate schedules are omitted. The estimate combines scalar work and
element traffic without fitted hardware weights; it is not a prediction of
absolute wall time, which requires native measurement.

The planner searches feasible source and channel widths under the same memory
bound. For each ket/channel choice it takes the largest feasible bra panel;
ties use fewer moment panels. Partial-channel residual sweeps currently use
one ket column because the shared forward workspace and moment buffers must
use the same channel width. Full-channel sweeps allow wider ket panels.
This is a workspace scheduling limit, not a restriction on the selected VB space.

\section{A shared accepted--tangent product sweep}\label{sec:product-jet}

For broad source support, retain the generated product contraction order
without retaining unbounded accepted or directional product arrays.
Write $F=\Ta\Wa$, $Y=F\vv C$, and let $\delta F$ denote the change of
the exterior map under the orbital direction. The complete directional
wave function and the accepted residual are
\begin{equation}
 Z=\delta F\vv C+F\delta\vv C,
 \qquad R_Y=(\Ha-E)Y.
\end{equation}
Here $E$ is held fixed in the orbital Lagrangian; its response and the
state-average gauge multipliers are included separately as in
Section~\ref{sec:factor-response}. Differentiating the complete projection,
rather than projecting an intermediate wave function, gives
\begin{align}
 \delta(F^*\Ha Y)&=F^*(\delta\Ha Y+\Ha Z)+(\delta F)^*\Ha Y,\label{eq:product-jet-H}\\
 \delta(F^*Y)&=F^*Z+(\delta F)^*Y.
 \label{eq:product-jet-images}
\end{align}
The left-map derivative is necessary even when $\delta\vv C=0$.
No invariance of the selected structure space is used.

With $\mathcal J$ and $\mathcal D$ defined above, the accepted and
directional orbital-generator cotangents are
\begin{align}
 \Gamma&=2\mathcal D(R_Y,Y),\\
 \delta\mathcal J&=2\mathcal J(Z,Y),\\
 \delta\Gamma&=2\left[
   \mathcal D(\Ha Z+\delta\Ha Y,Y)
   +\mathcal D(R_Y,Z)-E\mathcal D(Z,Y)\right].
 \label{eq:product-jet-generator}
\end{align}
Only the upper triangle of the final generator cotangent is needed by
the Cholesky pullback. The previously proved support certificate permits
$R_Y$ and the emitted Hamiltonian images to be restricted to $S$ for these
upper cotangents. It does not permit replacing a general oriented transition
density by its upper triangle.

If compact accepted panels of $(Y,R_Y)$ are resident, the same panels supply
the right readers, $\mathcal D(R_Y,Z)$, and
$\Ha Y=R_Y+EY$ in the left projection of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:product-jet-H}. Thus a coefficient-only response
requires neither another accepted exterior transform nor another accepted
Hamiltonian action. The channel sweep producing $\Ha Z$ also accumulates
$\mathcal J(Z,Y)$ before its slabs are released.
The forward orbital-direction consumer retains the resulting small
cotangents and prepared integral tangent; its reverse consumer reuses them.

When accepted panels cannot all remain resident under the shared budget,
generate $Y$ and $Z$ together and apply $\Ha$ to this two-column-family
block. The equivalent unretained form of the generator response is
\begin{equation}
 \delta\Gamma=2\left[
 \mathcal D(\Ha Z+\delta\Ha Y,Y)+\mathcal D(\Ha Y,Z)
 -E\{\mathcal D(Z,Y)+\mathcal D(Y,Z)\}\right].
\end{equation}
For real wave functions the last brace equals
$\mathcal D(Z,Y)+\mathcal D(Z,Y)\Tr$. One complete oriented traversal
therefore supplies both terms, followed by upper-triangle selection.
The resident and streamed equations are identical; residency changes only
reuse, not the variational space or accuracy. This does not assert that
all accepted work can be retained at fixed memory without recomputation.

Each emitted Hamiltonian panel is consumed by the structure projection and
generator adjoint before release. Partial images emitted on eviction are
valid because both consumers are linear. A one-body generator's neighbor
columns are themselves read in bounded batches; their union must not become
a complete right carrier. With panel width $b$, shared residency bound $B$
and the existing bounded sigma workspace $W_\sigma$, auxiliary numerical
storage, excluding immutable incidence and one-spin topology, is
\begin{equation}
 O\left(t+(U_\alpha+U_\beta)b+B+W_\sigma+n^4\right)
\end{equation}
per sequentially processed state, in addition to the selected coefficient
block. The $n^4$ term denotes the retained active integral and adjoint tensors,
not a spin-product array. If original touched support is complete, $t$
itself can equal $U_\alpha U_\beta$; this bound does not conceal that input
cost. Accepted residency is shared across roots, never copied per worker.

\section{Necessary projections and cancelling coordinate maps}
\label{sec:necessary-maps}

An overlap solve is not a physical observable. Its necessity depends on
the coordinate map that the optimizer has chosen. Let
$F(p)=\Ta(p)\Wa$ map selected structure coefficients into an orthogonal
spin-string carrier at orbital displacement $p$. With an independent
structure displacement $q$, write $\vv C_b=\vv C_0+Zq$, where $Z$ embeds
the structure tangent. The current finite CI transport projects the target
\begin{equation}
 b(p,q)=F(0)\vv C_b+\dot F(0)[p]\vv C_0
\end{equation}
onto the selected space at the displaced orbitals. Its normal equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-normal}
 M(p)\vv C(p,q)=F(p)\Tr b(p,q),\qquad M(p)=F(p)\Tr F(p).
\end{equation}
For a nonclosed selection this is a genuine restricted projection: replacing
$M(p)$ by the identity, or using an inverse on a larger complete space,
does not give the same transport. Its gradient pullback involves the adjoint
solve with the same $M(p)$. These operations are necessary for this finite
coordinate map, not for every possible VBSCF optimization framework.
Changing the map requires deriving its derivatives consistently, rather
than independently deleting the projection from a keyframe or an HVP.
Two requests for exactly the same $(p,q)$ within one immutable macro model,
however, solve exactly the same equation; the previously computed point is
sufficient for both consumers.

There is a stronger reduction when $\ran\Wa$ is a complete spin sector.
Define its orthogonal projector $P$ and its orbital-independent Gram $G$ by
\begin{equation}
 G=\Wa^*\Wa,\qquad P=\Wa G^{-1}\Wa^*.
\end{equation}
The common spatial orbital transformation commutes with the total-spin
projector. Thus $\Ta P=P\Ta$, as do its inverse and adjoint, and the
Moore--Penrose inverse of $F=\Ta\Wa$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-left-inverse}
 F^+=G^{-1}\Wa^*\Ta^{-1},\qquad
 FM^{-1}=\Ta^{-*}\Wa G^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the proposed left inverse times $F$ is the identity, while
$FF^+=\Ta P\Ta^{-1}=P$ is an orthogonal projector. Completeness here
means completeness of the selected spin sector, not merely completeness of
the ambient alpha and beta string lists. Covalent-only and arbitrary
selected spaces generally do not meet this hypothesis.

Let $D$ be the existing carrier diagonal, including its root-energy shift
and the unchanged denominator protection. The symmetric structure-space
inverse preconditioner can therefore be evaluated as
\begin{align}
 M^{-1}F^*D^{-1}FM^{-1}
 &=G^{-1}\Wa^*\Ta^{-1}D^{-1}\Ta^{-*}\Wa G^{-1}.
 \label{eq:cancelled-carrier-inverse}
\end{align}
No commutation between $D$ and the spin projector is needed: the two outer
structure maps retain the required projection. Equation~\eqref{eq:cancelled-carrier-inverse}
removes the interior inverse/forward exterior maps and repeated expansion
and contraction. It retains the same two Riesz maps algebraically; it does
not replace covectors by coefficient vectors. Only one carrier workspace
is live, with the original right-hand-side chunk bound. $G$ is factored by
spatial occupation blocks, not as a global dense structure matrix.

The normalization and selected-state constraints do not restore the need
for repeated metric solves. If the columns of $C$ are the selected states,
put $K=C\Tr MC$ and define
\begin{equation}
 A=M^{-1}-CK^{-1}C\Tr,\quad
 P_R=I-MCK^{-1}C\Tr,\quad P_L=P_R\Tr.
\end{equation}
The exact constrained inverse preconditioner is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-carrier-inverse}
 A F^*D^{-1}F A
 =P_L\left[M^{-1}F^*D^{-1}FM^{-1}\right]P_R.
\end{equation}
Only the already available $MC$ and the small state Gram $K$ are needed
for the two outer corrections. Thus two coordinate-metric CG solves in
the composite evaluation are unnecessary. Multiplication by the independent
horizontal coordinate basis before and after this expression gives the
same constrained operator. The identity applies columnwise when different
roots use different shifted carrier diagonals.

The same closure identity permits direct evaluation of the complete-sector
transport as
\begin{equation}
 \vv C=G^{-1}\Wa^*\Ta(p)^{-1}b.
\end{equation}
This does not license the
corresponding replacement in Eq.~\eqref{eq:transport-normal} for a nonclosed
selection. Moreover, CI transport used only as an initial guess for a later
fully relaxed root solve is distinct from the transport used to define a
keyframe objective: altering the former can change iteration cost and root
tracking, while altering the latter also changes the finite model and its
pullback. These two roles must not be conflated.

\subsection{Complete-sector CI coordinates}

For a complete spin sector, the orbital map need not appear in any inner
CI iteration. Factor the orbital-independent spin Gram by occupation blocks,
$G=LL\Tr$, and put
\begin{equation}
 U=\Wa L^{-\mathsf T},\qquad U\Tr U=I,\qquad UU\Tr=P.
\end{equation}
Here $L$ is block diagonal in spatial occupations; no global dense factor is
formed. The square change of selected-space coordinates and its inverse are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-ci-coordinates}
 J=U\Tr\Ta\Wa,\qquad
 J^{-1}=G^{-1}\Wa\Tr\Ta^{-1}U.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $UJ=\Ta\Wa$ follows from $P\Ta\Wa=\Ta\Wa$.
Consequently, the original generalized eigenproblem is exactly equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 H\vv C=M\vv C E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (U\Tr\Ha U)\vv x=\vv x E,\qquad \vv x=J\vv C.
\end{equation}
The dimension remains the number of independent selected spin-adapted
structures, not the dimension of the ambient spin-string product carrier.
An inner Hamiltonian action is $\vv x\mapsto U\Tr\Ha U\vv x$;
its overlap action is the identity. Thus no inner exterior orbital map or
orbital-dependent overlap action is mathematically needed.
In the shared Davidson solver, the overlap images of the trial basis are
then the trial basis itself. Their third stored basis matrix, associated
matrix--vector products and separate reorthogonalization updates are omitted.
The original subspace capacity is retained rather than spending the freed
memory on a larger subspace.

The existing complete-sector inverse preconditioner transforms by congruence
to $U\Tr D^{-1}U$. In particular, $r_C=J\Tr r_x$ and
$JQ_CJ\Tr=U\Tr D^{-1}U$ imply that the preconditioned correction is the
same physical direction. Metric orthogonalization of the original trial
vectors is ordinary orthogonalization of their images under $J$. The change
of coordinates therefore does not replace Davidson by a different physical
subspace algorithm. For a pure target-spin residual, its Euclidean norm in
these coordinates is its physical carrier norm. The same requested physical
stationarity accuracy is retained; a new relaxed threshold is not introduced.
The algebraic backward-error gates are evaluated in the isometric coordinates
with the same numerical tolerances. Those gates are coordinate dependent, so
the last accepted CI iteration need not be identical to that in the original
coefficient representation; the physical stationarity norm is invariant.
Initial trial vectors must first be mapped with $J$, rather than reinterpreted
as coordinates in a different basis. At the exit, return
\begin{equation}
 \vv C=J^{-1}\vv x,\qquad HC=J\Tr h,\qquad MC=J\Tr\vv x,
 \qquad h=(U\Tr\Ha U)\vv x.
\end{equation}
The retained Ritz images supply $h$ without another Hamiltonian application.
Only the boundary maps use $\Ta$ or its inverse. Carrier arrays are processed
in budgeted RHS chunks and the sigma contraction retains its bounded internal
tiles. No additional carrier-sized array is stored for each thread. This
elimination requires the complete-sector identity; it is not valid for a
general nonclosed selected VB space.

\subsection{Cross-overlap generator moments}
\label{sec:cross-moments}

CI transport also requires overlaps between two orbital representations.
Write $T_s=T_{s\alpha}\otimes T_{s\beta}$, where the exterior maps on
side $s\in\{l,r\}$ are induced by the same spatial upper-triangular
matrix $R_s$ for both spins. For arbitrary selected coefficients, define
\begin{equation}
 L=T_l\Wa c_l,\qquad R=T_r\Wa c_r,\qquad
 f=L\Tr R.
\end{equation}
These physical amplitudes lie in the ambient spin-string product carrier;
no closure of $\operatorname{range}(\Wa)$ under $T_s$ is assumed.
The cross-overlap action can be evaluated in the exact order
\begin{equation}
 c\longmapsto\Wa\Tr T_l\Tr[T_r\Wa c].
 \label{eq:cross-direct}
\end{equation}
Each exterior map and its directional derivative is applied once to the
shared amplitude array. In particular, it is unnecessary to reconstruct
every output product from all source columns after the transforms.

The adjoint admits an additional contraction reduction. Let
$\hat E_{pq}=\hat a^\dagger_{p\alpha}\hat a_{q\alpha}
+\hat a^\dagger_{p\beta}\hat a_{q\beta}$ and define
\begin{equation}
 D_{pq}=\langle R|\hat E_{pq}|L\rangle,\qquad
 K_s=\delta R_sR_s^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The representation derivatives are
$\delta L=\hat E(K_l)L$ and $\delta R=\hat E(K_r)R$, with
$\hat E(K)=\sum_{pq}K_{pq}\hat E_{pq}$. Thus the upper-triangular
orbital adjoints are
\begin{equation}
 g_l=\operatorname{upper}(DR_l^{-T}),\qquad
 g_r=\operatorname{upper}(D\Tr R_r^{-T}).
 \label{eq:cross-moment-adjoint}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{upper}$ retains the diagonal and upper triangle;
the transpose on $D$ follows from real one-body matrix elements, not from
equality of the left and right states. For a simultaneous direction,
\begin{align}
 \delta D_{pq}&=\langle\delta R|\hat E_{pq}|L\rangle
                 +\langle R|\hat E_{pq}|\delta L\rangle,\\
 \delta g_l&=\operatorname{upper}\bigl[
   \delta D R_l^{-T}-DR_l^{-T}(\delta R_l)\Tr R_l^{-T}\bigr],\\
 \delta g_r&=\operatorname{upper}\bigl[
   (\delta D)\Tr R_r^{-T}
   -D\Tr R_r^{-T}(\delta R_r)\Tr R_r^{-T}\bigr].
 \label{eq:cross-moment-jet}
\end{align}
Only one-body Slater--Condon links and small orbital triangular solves are
needed. No Hamiltonian sigma, cofactor, or extra structure projection is
required for these adjoints. Residual-weighted transport curvature uses
these identities without setting the finite CI Ritz residual to zero.

If $A$ and $B$ denote the numbers of spin strings and $r$ the number of
source columns, the source-factor adjoint includes reconstruction work
$O(|\Omega|r)$, in addition to its exterior transforms and generator
contractions. For broad touched support, $|\Omega|=AB$ and $r=B$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:cross-direct}--\eqref{eq:cross-moment-jet} replace that
reconstruction by $O(\operatorname{nnz}\Wa+n_{\rm act}^2 AB)$ work.
This is a different exact contraction order, not a low-rank truncation.
It requires one or two shared carrier arrays for the overlap action,
and two to four for its adjoint jet, depending on which side varies.
They are admitted only within the existing caller workspace budget;
there are no per-thread carrier copies. Narrow touched support or a
carrier exceeding that budget retains the bounded source-panel order.
Independent spin-dependent orbital maps also use that order, since
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-moment-adjoint} combines the two spins only for a
common spatial chart. Neither schedule changes the selected VB space.

The active-space forward entries complete the computational fixed-spin
string tables before constructing the action. Original input incidences are
remapped into those tables; neither the selected VB structures nor their
independent coefficients are enlarged. This completion supplies the exterior
maps and Slater--Condon links without allocating numerical pair tables or
product amplitudes. It must occur in the production entries, not only in
selected-space test fixtures.

Fixed-point structure Hamiltonian/overlap actions choose between the common
factor sweep and generated product tiles. Accepted-state integral gradients
choose between the exact factor and
product contractions by their arithmetic costs. Raw state coefficients are
retained in structure space. Broad product-order accepted, directional and
reverse consumers use Section~\ref{sec:product-jet}. Compact accepted
wave-function/residual panels may remain under one shared residency cap;
unbounded accepted and directional carrier fields are deleted.
A selected-space eigenvector need not be
stationary on the full carrier: its residual contribution to the
exterior-transform gradient is retained.
Accepted-state transition densities use Eq.~\eqref{eq:generator-factor}
when advantageous. The residual-panel kernel of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual-panels} is available and independently tested;
source-order accepted states now omit accepted orthogonal and residual arrays.
The coupled coefficient-response consumer in
Section~\ref{sec:factor-response} uses neither accepted nor response product
arrays when its factor contraction order is cheaper. Section~\ref{sec:source-state}
extends that ownership to source-state orbital directions and HVPs. Broad
product-order derivative consumers now share the generated product jet.
Complete-space inverse/preconditioner consumers remain a separate ownership boundary.

\paragraph{Main result.}
Equations~\eqref{eq:Hfactor}--\eqref{eq:final} give the exact
selected-space restriction through spin factors and Slater--Condon actions,
without a closure assumption or a complete transformed product array.
