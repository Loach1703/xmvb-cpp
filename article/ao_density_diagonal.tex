\section{Required quantities}

Let $U\in\mathbb R^{N\times m}$ be the AO-coefficient frame used for the
analytic orbital diagonal, where $N$ is the number of AO basis functions.
The current complete frame has $m=N$; the identities below also hold for
a rectangular frame. For a symmetric, not necessarily positive density
$D$, define
\begin{align}
 J[D]_{\mu\nu}&=\sum_{\kappa\lambda}
       (\mu\nu|\kappa\lambda)D_{\kappa\lambda},\\
 K[D]_{\mu\nu}&=\sum_{\kappa\lambda}
       (\mu\kappa|\nu\lambda)D_{\kappa\lambda}.
\end{align}
The analytic diagonal requires projected scalar responses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diag}
 j_p[D]=U_p\T J[D]U_p,\qquad
 k_p[D]=U_p\T K[D]U_p,
\end{equation}
not the full AO matrices $J[D]$ and $K[D]$. Here $U_p$ is column $p$ of
$U$. For an inactive orbital $c$, $D_c=U_cU_c\T$. Both $j_p[D_c]$ and
$k_p[D_c]$ enter the signed analytic curvature, including the combination
$6k_p[D_c]-2j_p[D_c]$. These contributions cannot be discarded simply
because the selected VB structure space is one-dimensional.

For the active orbitals $A\in\mathbb R^{N\times n}$, all required density
sources have the form $D_t=A W_t A\T$, where $W_t$ is a symmetric active
matrix constructed from the accepted spin-free one- and two-particle
integral adjoints. The existing untruncated source-span factorization
requires $r=\min(n(n+1)/2,2n)$ basis sources. Its reconstruction matrix
recovers the original $2n$ responses and their total one-particle source.
No additional numerical rank test or approximation is introduced here.

\section{Coulomb responses already available}

The accepted integral preparation supplies
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_{p,ab}=(pp|ab).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-j}
 j_p[D_t]=\sum_{ab}\mathcal J_{p,ab}(W_t)_{ab}.
\end{equation}
Recomputing $J[D_t]$ through the AO integral graph is unnecessary.
Only the active exchange response must still be contracted. The physical
response $2j_p[D_t]-k_p[D_t]$ is formed after Eq.~\eqref{eq:known-j}.
The corresponding inactive Coulomb projections are not present in this
active tensor and must be evaluated; they can share the exchange traversal.

\section{Immediate row projection}

At a fixed AO index $\mu$, collect the required exchange rows and inactive
Coulomb rows in one matrix,
\begin{equation}
 R_\mu(\nu,t)=K[D_t]_{\mu\nu},\qquad
 R_\mu(\nu,b+c)=J[D_c]_{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
where $b$ is the current source batch width, and $c$ labels its $b_c$
inactive sources. There are $s=b+b_c\leq2b$ columns. Once this AO row is
complete, compute
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
 B_\mu=U\T R_\mu,\qquad
 d_{pt}=\sum_\mu U_{\mu p}(B_\mu)_{pt}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:row} is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:diag}, with the contraction
order changed. It is not a diagonal approximation to an AO operator.
The entire row panel is projected by one matrix multiplication and released
before the next owned row. No full AO response, per-channel AO staging
image, or second core-Coulomb AO-pair transformation is needed.

Each OpenMP worker owns its AO row panel, projected row panel and partial
diagonals. Workers borrow the same densities, integral graph and frame.
The numerical row workspace is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memory}
 8T(N+2m)(b+b_c)\ \text{bytes},
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the worker count. Including a shared source batch, source-building
scratch and required small outputs gives
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl(N^2b+N^2+T(N+2m)b+mP\bigr),
\end{equation}
for $P=n_{\mathrm{core}}+r$ sources. In particular, there is no worker-owned
$N\times N$ response. Batch width and, when necessary, worker count are
chosen within the existing workspace allowance. These choices affect only
the exact contraction order and residency, never the physical state space.

The shared density batch remains necessary for this row contraction.
Its replacement by a generated or factored source would require a separate
algorithm; it is not claimed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:row}. The arithmetic order
$O(N^2mP)$ of the MO projection is also unchanged. The present reductions
are redundant Coulomb work, response storage/copying, synchronization and
repeated integral visits, not a new asymptotic integral factorization.

\section{Integral traversal and batching}

For a symmetric AO-pair CSR graph, let $e$ be its directed edge count and
$d_\mu$ the length of the diagonal pair row $(\mu,\mu)$. One traversal of
all ordered AO output rows visits
\begin{equation}
 R=2e-\sum_\mu d_\mu
\end{equation}
raw CSR records. If $I$ is the number of permutationally unique integrals,
the canonical packed-scatter layout visits
$I\lceil P/b_{\mathrm{stream}}\rceil$ records, whereas the projected row
layout visits $R\lceil P/b\rceil$. These are record counts, not complete
FLOP or wall-time estimates: the layouts exploit integral symmetry differently.
Both exact storage orders remain useful, including the canonical stream when
the integral graph cannot use 32-bit CSR addresses.

If all $P$ channels fit the row workspace, the final SIMD remainder belongs
in the same traversal. Replacing $P=52$ by a SIMD-aligned batch of 48 would
force a second pass over the entire graph without a mathematical need.
Alignment of nonfinal batches is useful; alignment must not create another
batch when all channels already fit.

For the LOFLEA CAS(12,12) input, $N=185$, $n_{\mathrm{core}}=28$, $r=24$
and $P=52$. The saved eight-wide canonical run visits 517,740,328 records.
A single projected row traversal visits 293,300,715. Actual speed and peak
RSS must still be measured under Slurm; the record reduction alone is not
a speedup measurement. Numerical validation compares the same signed
analytic diagonal against independently transformed four-index integrals,
including indefinite sources, repeated AO indices and zero-integral graphs.
